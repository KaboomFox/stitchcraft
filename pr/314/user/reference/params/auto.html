StitchCraft

Auto parameters

Automatic digitizing: the settings of the rules that choose the stitches of a shape without settings from its geometry. Each rule goes by the settings of the shape it digitizes, and a shape that sets none takes the defaults, as every shape does with --auto. The machine checkpoint MC-8 confirms the defaults or changes them. The settings of the last section, Advanced, are the rules’ bounds: no dialog shows them, and stitch plan --set sets them.

Applies to automatic elements, the shapes whose stitches StitchCraft chooses by their width, before they are sewn as any stitch type.

Stitches by shape

auto_stitch

Stitch. The stitch the shape sews as. Left at auto, each part of it sews by the first rule that it matches, from its geometry. run sews every part as a running stitch along its centre lines, satin sews each part no wider than the widest satin column as satin columns along them, and fill fills the shape whole. A part that can’t sew as asked goes by the rules: a speck, a part without a centre line, and for satin a part too narrow or too wide for a column.

  • Accepts: one of auto (Chosen by the rules), run (Running stitch), satin (Satin columns), fill (Tatami fill)
  • Default: auto
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_prefer

Satin or fill. Which way the rules lean where a part could sew as satin columns or as a fill. satin moves 3 bounds together by half, so that more parts sew as columns: the least slenderness is divided by 1.5, and the most a width varies and the narrowest piece that stays in a fill are multiplied by it. fill moves them the other way, and balanced leaves them as they are set.

  • Accepts: one of satin (Prefer satin columns), balanced (Balanced), fill (Prefer fills)
  • Default: balanced
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_fill_uneven

Fill uneven parts. Whether a part of several centre lines whose width varies along them is filled. On, such a part is a tatami fill: the ragged patches of a traced photo sew flat, and a ragged thin line merges into the area beside it. Off, it sews a satin column along each line, and a thin line runs along its middle, as a drawing made of strokes of changing width may want.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: true
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_fill_angle

Fill angle. The angle of the rows of every fill that automatic digitizing makes, as a fill’s angle gives it. One angle for the whole design lets a small filled part lie flat beside the fills round it.

  • Accepts: an angle in degrees
  • Default: 0
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_fill_outline

Outline round fills. Whether each fill that automatic digitizing makes sews an outline: a running stitch round its edge, after its rows, in its own thread. The outline draws the edge of a small shape as one line, where the ends of its rows would leave its points blunt. It sews only where the edge shows: not where a shape sewn after the fill covers the edge. The design goes by the setting of its lowest automatic shape.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: true
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

Neighbours

auto_overlap_mm

Overlap. How far an area reaches under each shape sewn after it that it touches, and how far inside the edge of an area, or of a part sewn as a line or a column, that is sewn after it and covers it the area is cut away. The thread pulls stitches in from the edges of their shape, and without the overlap fabric shows between shapes that only meet. A satin column reaches as far past a rail that a shape sewn after it runs along.

  • Accepts: a length from 0 to 2 mm
  • Default: 1
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_pull_compensation_mm

Pull compensation. How far a satin column sews past a rail with no shape sewn after the column beside it: its pull compensation there. The thread pulls a column in from its rails, and a column that sews this far past them is as wide as drawn. Along a rail where a shape sewn after the column runs for more than half the rail’s length, the column sews past the rail by the overlap instead.

  • Accepts: a length from 0 to 2 mm
  • Default: 0.4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_ignore_background

Leave out the background. Whether the lowest shape is left out when it is the picture’s background: an area under the other shapes that covers the whole design. A background is usually the fabric’s own colour, and sewing it doubles the stitches and stiffens the design. What shows of it inside the picture sews still, as a white disc on a badge traced with its colours stacked does. Off, all of it that shows sews, as of any area.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: true
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

Base layer

auto_base_layer

Base layer. Whether one base layer sews first under the whole design: an open fill under what the automatic shapes paint together, every stitch of it underlay. On fabric of another colour than the design, thread of one colour then lies under a slit between 2 shapes. The fills on it sew no underlay of their own. Off, each fill sews the underlay of its settings. The design goes by the settings its lowest automatic shape has for the base layer.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: true
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_stitch_length_mm

Base layer’s stitch length. How long the base layer’s stitches are. Its rows lie this length times 0.866 apart, so that its needle points make triangles with sides this long, as the stitches of Ink/Stitch’s Knockdown Fill do. Shorter stitches hide more of the fabric and sew longer.

  • Accepts: a length from 1 to 6 mm
  • Default: 3
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_keep_holes

Base layer keeps holes. Whether the base layer keeps the holes of the design’s outer shape. Off, it sews across them.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: true
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

Advanced

auto_max_satin_width_mm

Widest satin column. The widest a line or a part of an area sews as a satin column, and the longest stitch such a column sews between its rails: satin stitches any longer lie loose. A line up to this wide is a satin column as wide as the line, and a wider one fills the area it paints. A part of an area wider than this somewhere is filled. As a traced picture’s layers are flattened, an area that is filled is filled whole under the areas that fill its holes. The value is the machine’s longest satin stitch, so a shape may set it lower and never higher.

  • Accepts: a length from 1 to 7 mm
  • Default: 7
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_triple_run_width_mm

Narrowest triple run. The narrowest running stitch sewn as a triple run, each stitch sewn 3 times (bean stitch 1). A line no wider than the design’s narrowest satin stroke runs, and from this width it sews bolder than one stitch, which is as fine as the thread. A narrow part of an area is as wide as the circles that fit inside it along its centre line.

  • Accepts: a length from 0 to 10 mm
  • Default: 0.5
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_slenderness

Least slenderness of a column. How long for its width a part is, at the least, to sew as satin columns: its area divided by its width squared. A part less slender is compact, a blob with no way to run, and is filled. A rectangle is as slender as it is long for its width, and a disc measures 0.8.

  • Accepts: a number from 0 to 20
  • Default: 3
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_max_unevenness

Most a column’s width varies. How much the width of a part of several centre lines varies along them, at the most, for it to sew as satin columns: the standard deviation of its width divided by its width. A part more uneven is filled, unless auto_fill_uneven is off. A part of one centre line isn’t held to it: a leaf and a tapering stroke sew well as one column.

  • Accepts: a number from 0 to 5
  • Default: 0.3
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_line_unevenness

Least a merged line’s width varies. How much the width of a thin line varies along it, at the least, for it to merge into the shape beside it (auto_fill_uneven): the standard deviation of its width divided by its width. A thin line is a part narrower than the narrowest satin stroke everywhere. The ragged strands that soft shading and fur trace into vary by 0.09 to 0.46, and merge. A line drawn with an even pen varies by less, and sews as a line.

  • Accepts: a number from 0 to 5
  • Default: 0.05
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_likeness

Least likeness kept when lines merge. The least likeness of the picture that merging thin lines keeps. A part is a piece of one colour of 1 mm² or more in which a disc a thread across fits, and the likeness is the mean over the colours of the share of their parts kept. A thin line that touches no other piece of its colour is a part of its own, and merged into a neighbour of another colour it is a part lost: a twig, the rim of a petal, a shadow. Such lines merge, those of the colours with the most parts and the smallest first, while the picture keeps this likeness, and the others sew as lines. At 0 every thin line merges, and at 1 none that is a part does. Keeping lines adds running stitches and jump threads, and it changes the order the blocks sew in: the cap (auto_thick_cap_fills) keeps the spots that order piles up within it. Lower it for fewer jump threads where the detail matters less.

  • Accepts: a number from 0 to 1
  • Default: 0.85
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_max_reach

Farthest a column reaches from its line. How far a part reaches from one of its centre lines on both sides of the line, at the most, for it to sew as satin columns: the distance, divided by the radius of the widest circle that fits inside the part centred on its lines. A column’s edges lie as far from its line as that circle reaches, so it measures 1, on one side or the other. A star measures 1.6 to 2.6: its centre line runs from one point to another, and its other points lie far from the line on both sides. One column along that line would sew them as bumps, so a part that reaches farther is filled. A sharp corner reaches far on one side only, and stays a column.

  • Accepts: a number from 1 to 20
  • Default: 1.4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_fill_width_mm

Narrowest piece of a fill. The narrowest piece of an area that stays in its fill. A piece narrower than this leaves the fill of an area wider than a satin column and sews by the rules, as a line or as columns. A piece as wide or wider stays, so that a wing 6 mm wide isn’t cut from the fill of its body.

  • Accepts: a length from 1 to 7 mm
  • Default: 4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_min_area_mm2

Smallest area. The smallest area sewn. An area, or a part of one, smaller than this is a speck, which would sew a knot: it is merged into the area beside it that it shares the longest edge with, or left out when it shares none.

  • Accepts: an area from 0 to 10 mm²
  • Default: 1
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_offset_mm

Base layer’s offset. How far outside the design’s outer shape the base layer’s edge lies: inside it when negative, so that no base stitch shows beside a shape once the thread has pulled the shape’s edge in. By default it lies as far inside as a satin column’s pull compensation reaches outside.

  • Accepts: a length from -3 to 3 mm
  • Default: -0.4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_rows

Base layer’s rows. Which way the base layer’s rows run. across sews one layer, its rows across those of the fills on it, auto_fill_angle turned by 90°: the underlay a fill is usually given, once under the whole design. lattice sews 3 layers 60° apart, as Ink/Stitch’s Knockdown Fill does. That holds down the pile of a towel or a fleece, and lays 3 times the thread under the design.

  • Accepts: one of across (Across the fills’ rows), lattice (In 3 directions)
  • Default: across
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_sliver_mm2

Largest sliver. The largest gap in the top thread that is left to the base layer: a sliver. Where thin shapes meet, the top thread can leave a place this small bare, and the base layer’s thread shows there in place of the fabric. Nothing sews differently by this value. It is a limit of the check of a design’s cover: a larger gap fails the check, and so does a gap with no base layer under it.

  • Accepts: an area from 0 to 2 mm²
  • Default: 0.4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_base_bare_percent

Most left to the base layer. The most of the design’s area that the top thread may leave bare over the base layer, the slivers and the places narrower than a thread together. Nothing sews differently by this value. It is the other limit of the check of a design’s cover. A design without a base layer is held to 0.15 % and to no gap at all.

  • Accepts: a percentage from 0 to 5
  • Default: 0.5
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_thick_warn_fills

Thread that warns, in fills. How many fills of thread over one spot make the design sew thick. A fill is what rows 0.4 mm apart lay, the width of the thread. Nothing sews differently by this value: planning warns of a place of 1 mm² or more with as much thread, which a person should look at. One layer of satin over a fill lays 2 to 3 fills and sews well, and 2 dense layers on a fill, 4, risk broken needles. It is a starting value for a 75/11 needle and 40-weight polyester thread, which a sew-out has to confirm. The design goes by the settings its lowest automatic shape has for the 4 limits of its thickness.

  • Accepts: a number from 0.5 to 50
  • Default: 4
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_thick_fail_fills

Thread that fails, in fills. How many fills of thread over one spot fail: the warning gives the area with as much, and the heat map draws it in its last colour. A value under the warning’s limit reads as that limit.

  • Accepts: a number from 0.5 to 50
  • Default: 6
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_thick_warn_points

Needle points that warn, per mm². How many needle points to a square millimetre make the design sew thick, the locks left out. Planning warns of a place of 1 mm² or more with as many. The rows of a fill end at its edge, 5 to a square millimetre there, so this is what the edges of 3 fills on each other make. A starting value, as the limit of thread.

  • Accepts: a number from 0.5 to 200
  • Default: 15
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_thick_fail_points

Needle points that fail, per mm². How many needle points to a square millimetre fail, what the edges of 6 fills on each other make: the warning gives the area with as many. A value under the warning’s limit reads as that limit.

  • Accepts: a number from 0.5 to 200
  • Default: 30
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_thick_cap_fills

Most thread on a spot, in fills. The most fills of thread a spot of a millimetre sews: what planning aims at. Where a spot sews more once the design is planned, a last pass and then the cap take thread there that other stitches cover: travel, the base layer, a shape’s thread under later shapes, and a shape’s own top stitches where they are doubled. The cap leaves a satin column’s top stitches as they are, adds no jump thread, and what shows keeps its colour. A note says what is left above the cap, and the warning comes only at its own limit. A starting value for a 75/11 needle and 40-weight polyester thread, which a sew-out has to confirm. The design goes by the setting of its lowest automatic shape.

  • Accepts: a number from 1 to 50
  • Default: 3.25
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version

auto_details_last

Details sew last. Whether a detail sews after the larger areas round it, where that costs no thread change: an automatic area that the shapes round it close in on, smaller than an area it meets, as an eye is in the fur round it. Sewn last, the detail keeps the later fills’ rows off its edge. Off by default: a fill that moves for a detail can wait for a later batch of its thread, which adds jump threads to cut by hand, and on traced photos it shows no more of the picture. Turn it on where an eye or a mark must keep its edge clear. The design goes by the setting of its lowest automatic shape.

  • Accepts: true or false, in any case, or yes, y, t or 1 for true and no, n, f, 0 or off for false
  • Default: false
  • Ink/Stitch: none; this is StitchCraft’s own
  • Experimental: designs that use it may stitch differently in a later version
