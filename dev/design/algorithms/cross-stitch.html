StitchCraft

Cross stitch

A cross in each cell of a grid laid over the fill, where the fill covers enough of the cell (P3, M10.1). Cross stitch is counted work. Hand embroiderers sew it on even-weave fabric, every cross the same size and in rows and columns. A shape is filled by choosing cells of the grid, and its edges step along the grid’s lines. StitchCraft sews Ink/Stitch’s simple crosses, plain and flipped, as Ink/Stitch sews them (read at d59c9ab, lib/stitches/cross_stitch.py, lib/stitches/utils/cross_stitch.py and the fill element’s cross stitch settings in lib/elements/fill_stitch.py, described here in our own words, ADR-0012). The other crosses, half, upright, double and Smyrna, arrive with M10.2, and the turned grid (cross_rotation) with it.

24 of the 25 cross-stitch fonts in Ink/Stitch’s font library sew simple crosses only, on grids of 1.5 to 3 mm with 2 or 4 threads. They draw each letter as rectangles of whole cells, grown by 0.1 mm, on a grid that starts at each rectangle’s corner (canvas_grid_origin off).

The pattern size and the offset are 1 length, for both ways, or 2 separated by a space. The registry’s LengthPair kind reads exactly that, as it reads a satin’s pull compensation for each of its sides, and they are of that kind. Ink/Stitch takes the first 2 of more values, where StitchCraft leaves the element out with SC-E0101 (DEV-PRM-004). Ink/Stitch reads a pattern size below 0.1 mm as 0.1, an offset below 0 as 0 and a coverage below 1 % as 1 %, and StitchCraft clamps them so too, with SC-W0102. Its coverage is a whole number, and its fonts write it as 50.0 too, which it sews at its default of 50. StitchCraft reads it as a percentage, and 50.0 is 50 here too (DEV-PRM-001).

The grid

Each part of the fill’s area is grown by expand_mm first. Grown parts that meet are one part, as Ink/Stitch grows the whole shape at once. Shrunk, each part is shrunk alone, and a part shrunk away is sewn at its own size, with SC-W0309, as every fill’s part is. Ink/Stitch keeps the shape as drawn only when nothing at all is left of it, and otherwise sews no cross in such a part (DEV-FILL-024).

The grid’s cells are pattern_size_mm wide and tall. Its lines pass through the canvas’s origin, so that 2 fills of cross stitch that lie side by side share one grid and their crosses line up. With canvas_grid_origin off, they pass through the top left corner of the part’s bounds instead, and the part sews the same wherever it lies. cross_offset_mm moves the lines left by its first length and down by its second, as Ink/Stitch moves them. A move by a whole cell leaves the grid where it was.

The canvas’s origin. In an SVG file it is the origin of the page, where Ink/Stitch puts it, and the SVG reader puts the page’s top left corner at the design’s origin. In a VectorCraft document it is the origin of the document’s coordinates, which the VectorCraft adapter keeps as the design’s origin (coordinates). A new document’s first artboard has its top left corner there. Moving an artboard then leaves the grid where it is, a fill and its neighbours on other artboards share it, and a document exported to SVG keeps it where its page puts the origin.

Every cell within the part’s bounds is looked at. Ink/Stitch starts at the grid’s first line at or above the part’s top, moved down by the offset’s second length, and never looks at the row above it. Where the part’s top lies less far below a line than that length, Ink/Stitch loses the row of crosses the part covers there. A 3 mm grid moved 2 mm down over a rectangle whose top lies 0.2 mm below a line leaves out a row the rectangle covers 60 % of (DEV-FILL-025).

Coverage

A cell sews a cross when the part covers fill_coverage percent of it or more. Ink/Stitch allows a ten-thousandth of a percent of slack, and StitchCraft allows the same. A cell covered exactly half sews at 50, however the arithmetic rounds. The covered area is worked out exactly, row by row. Each of the part’s rings is clipped to the row’s strip, as Sutherland and Hodgman clip a polygon by each of the strip’s edges. By Green’s theorem, the area of the clipped rings left of a line down the strip is a sum over their sides, of the area between each side and the strip’s top edge. Each side’s share then falls to the columns it crosses. The holes run the other way round, and take their area away. A part that covers no cell so much sews nothing, and SC-W0319 says so, as Ink/Stitch warns of it.

Every cell within the bounds costs a unit of work before any is looked at. A row of cells, held while they are looked at, and each cell that sews a cross must fit in the stitches left (REQ-GEN-004). A grid of 0.1 mm over a part 1 m wide holds 10⁸ cells, and ends with SC-E0004 before it takes memory.

A cross

Each cross is sewn as a loop from one of its corners back to it. Its \ diagonal goes under and its / diagonal over, as the drawing shows them, and between the 2 the needle passes through the cell’s middle, where the second diagonal covers it. Each half of each diagonal takes cross_thread_count passes of the thread, the needle going back and forth along it, and the count is even. An odd count sews the even count below it, as in Ink/Stitch, and 1 sews 2, with SC-W0320. Ink/Stitch sews a stitch from a corner of each cell to its middle for 1, and no cross (DEV-FILL-027).

From a corner of its / diagonal, the loop runs in to the middle along that diagonal, which it sews last. It then sews the \ diagonal back and forth from the corner on the start’s side, the top or the bottom, returns to the middle, and sews the / diagonal from its far end back to the start. Nothing of the loop lies over the / diagonal. From a corner of its \ diagonal, the loop first sews that diagonal back and forth, ending at its far end. It goes in to the middle and sews the / diagonal from its top, then returns through the middle to where it started. That last stitch lies over the / diagonal.

simple_cross_flipped sews the / diagonal first and the \ diagonal over it, on the same grid. A flipped cross is a plain cross turned a quarter turn. The part, its grid, the needle and the end are turned, sewn as plain crosses, and turned back, exactly. Ink/Stitch turns the part alone and lays its grid afresh over it. Its flipped cells are then the pattern size’s second length wide and its first tall, the offset’s second length moves them right and its first down, and with canvas_grid_origin off its grid starts at the part’s bottom left corner (DEV-FILL-026). On square cells, without an offset and on the canvas’s grid, both lay the same crosses.

Sewing the crosses

Crosses that share a corner, side by side or corner to corner, form a group, and each group is one run, from a corner back to it. The run starts with a cross at its corner, one whose / diagonal ends there if it has one, and goes round its loop. At each corner along the run, before going on, it sews the loop of each cross not sewn yet whose / diagonal ends there. The cross below and to the left of the corner goes first and the one above and to the right second, each loop sewn the same way as the run goes along it. Such a loop starts along its own / diagonal, and nothing of it lies on top. Where the run has been round and crosses are left, which only their \ diagonals’ ends join to it, it takes the first corner along it where such a cross has one, the cross below and to the right first. It sews that loop there, ending on top, and goes on as before. Every corner of a cross sewn lies on the run, and every cross of the group is sewn.

The groups go in this order:

  • The group with the corner nearest the end is sewn last, from that corner, and the part ends there. The end is the next part’s point nearest this one, or for the last part the next element’s offer, or the end position.
  • The others go first, each the group with the corner nearest the needle, from that corner, the needle being where the run before it ended. With no needle, the first is the group of the first cross, row by row from the top, from that cross’s top right corner.
  • A part of one group with both a needle and an end first runs from its corner nearest the needle to its corner nearest the end through the middles of the fewest crosses. Its loops start from there, and the crosses sewn later cover these stitches.

Each run is a group of stitches of its own, which assembly joins to the next with a stitch when it starts within the element’s jump length, and with a jump otherwise, as Ink/Stitch’s stitch plan joins its runs. The parts are sewn nearest the needle first, as a tatami fill’s are, and a fill with a start position starts its first part nearest it and each part after nearest where the part before ended (DEV-FILL-023).

How it compares with Ink/Stitch. Ink/Stitch builds the same runs, of the same loops. It takes its groups, and the crosses that meet at a corner, in the order of a Python set of its objects, which follows where they lie in memory, where StitchCraft’s order is fixed. Where the needle and the end lie nearest one group of several, it sews another group first, from that group’s corner nearest the end. And with neither, it starts each group at the first corner it finds of one cross alone (DEV-FILL-028). The crosses and their stitches are the same in both, and the measures of the differential agree.

Stitches

Each place of a run is a needle point. A stitch longer than max_cross_stitch_length_mm is split into the fewest equal stitches no longer, as Ink/Stitch’s geometry library splits it, and a stitch exactly as long is not split. A longest stitch below twice the shortest stitch is raised to it, with SC-W0402, as a fill’s is, and every piece of a split stitch is at least the shortest stitch. A cross’s shortest stitches run from its corners to its middle, half its diagonal. On a grid so fine that they are shorter than the shortest stitch, SC-W0321 says so, and a needle point nearer the one before than the shortest stitch is left out, as finalize would leave it out.

A cross stitch fill sews its crosses alone, without the underlay, pull compensation or random stitch lengths of a tatami fill, as in Ink/Stitch. A preview too costly to sew shows each cross’s 2 diagonals.

Test sheet

TS-17A sews squares 24 mm wide on grids of 2 and 3 mm, with 2 and 4 threads, plain and flipped, and a K whose slanted edges cut their cells in half, with the fonts’ 2 threads and 0.1 mm of growth (machine testing). Every sample lies on the canvas’s grid, its corner a multiple of 6 mm from the origin, so that the squares sew whole cells. VectorCraft’s Test Sheet filter moves the sheet by whole multiples of 6 mm, which keep them. The differential compares the sheet with Ink/Stitch’s sewing of it (REQ-FILL-CRS-007).
