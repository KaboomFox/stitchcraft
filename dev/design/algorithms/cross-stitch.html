StitchCraft

Cross stitch

A cross in each cell of a grid laid over the fill, where the fill covers enough of the cell (P3, M10.1 and M10.2). Cross stitch is counted work. Hand embroiderers sew it on even-weave fabric, every cross the same size and in rows and columns. A shape is filled by choosing cells of the grid, and its edges step along the grid’s lines. StitchCraft sews Ink/Stitch’s 12 cross stitch methods as Ink/Stitch sews them (read at d59c9ab, lib/stitches/cross_stitch.py, lib/stitches/cross_stitch_half.py, lib/stitches/utils/cross_stitch.py and the fill element’s cross stitch settings in lib/elements/fill_stitch.py, described here in our own words, ADR-0012). Simple, upright, dense upright, double and Smyrna crosses are full crosses, a stack of bars through each cell’s middle, sewn in runs of loops. Half crosses sew one diagonal in each cell, in rows.

24 of the 25 cross-stitch fonts in Ink/Stitch’s font library sew simple crosses only, on grids of 1.5 to 3 mm with 2 or 4 threads. They draw each letter as rectangles of whole cells, grown by 0.1 mm, on a grid that starts at each rectangle’s corner (canvas_grid_origin off). Jersey 15, the 25th, sews its 238 elements as double crosses of 2 threads, grown so and on such a grid, of the default 3 mm cells.

The pattern size and the offset are 1 length, for both ways, or 2 separated by a space. The registry’s LengthPair kind reads exactly that, as it reads a satin’s pull compensation for each of its sides, and they are of that kind. Ink/Stitch takes the first 2 of more values, where StitchCraft leaves the element out with SC-E0101 (DEV-PRM-004). Ink/Stitch reads a pattern size below 0.1 mm as 0.1, an offset below 0 as 0 and a coverage below 1 % as 1 %, and StitchCraft clamps them so too, with SC-W0102. Its coverage is a whole number, and its fonts write it as 50.0 too, which it sews at its default of 50. StitchCraft reads it as a percentage, and 50.0 is 50 here too (DEV-PRM-001). The rotation is an angle of the registry’s Angle kind, read in (−180°, 180°], so a whole turn is no turn.

The grid

Each part of the fill’s area is grown by expand_mm first. Grown parts that meet are one part, as Ink/Stitch grows the whole shape at once. Shrunk, each part is shrunk alone, and a part shrunk away is sewn at its own size, with SC-W0309, as every fill’s part is. Ink/Stitch keeps the shape as drawn only when nothing at all is left of it, and otherwise sews no cross in such a part (DEV-FILL-024).

The grid’s cells are pattern_size_mm wide and tall. Its lines pass through the canvas’s origin, so that 2 fills of cross stitch that lie side by side share one grid and their crosses line up. With canvas_grid_origin off, they pass through the top left corner of the part’s bounds instead, and the part sews the same wherever it lies. cross_offset_mm moves the lines left by its first length and down by its second, as Ink/Stitch moves them. A move by a whole cell leaves the grid where it was.

The canvas’s origin. In an SVG file it is the origin of the page, where Ink/Stitch puts it, and the SVG reader puts the page’s top left corner at the design’s origin. In a VectorCraft document it is the origin of the document’s coordinates, which the VectorCraft adapter keeps as the design’s origin (coordinates). A new document’s first artboard has its top left corner there. Moving an artboard then leaves the grid where it is, a fill and its neighbours on other artboards share it, and a document exported to SVG keeps it where its page puts the origin.

Every cell within the part’s bounds is looked at. Ink/Stitch starts at the grid’s first line at or above the part’s top, moved down by the offset’s second length, and never looks at the row above it. Where the part’s top lies less far below a line than that length, Ink/Stitch loses the row of crosses the part covers there. A 3 mm grid moved 2 mm down over a rectangle whose top lies 0.2 mm below a line leaves out a row the rectangle covers 60 % of (DEV-FILL-025).

The turned grid

cross_rotation turns the grid. The part is turned the other way, the grid is laid over the part so turned as above, the crosses are sewn there, and their stitches are turned back. On the canvas’s grid the turn is about the point (x, y) that the offset’s lengths name, as Ink/Stitch turns it, where the grid’s own lines pass through (−x, y). Fills side by side with the same turn and offset then share one turned grid. With canvas_grid_origin off the grid starts at the turned part’s top left corner, so the point the grid turns about changes nothing but rounding. StitchCraft turns such a grid about the canvas’s origin. Ink/Stitch turns it about the part’s bottom left corner, or about the point that a file’s inkscape:transform-center-x and -y name.

That is how lettering along a path turns a glyph of a cross-stitch font (M14.3). Ink/Stitch’s lettering turns each glyph to follow the path and sets its elements’ cross_rotation to the same angle, so a letter drawn as whole cells keeps its crosses square to its sides. A whole number of quarter turns turns each point exactly, and a whole turn turns nothing.

Coverage

A cell sews a cross when the part covers fill_coverage percent of it or more. Ink/Stitch allows a ten-thousandth of a percent of slack, and StitchCraft allows the same. A cell covered exactly half sews at 50, however the arithmetic rounds. The covered area is worked out exactly, row by row. Each of the part’s rings is clipped to the row’s strip, as Sutherland and Hodgman clip a polygon by each of the strip’s edges. By Green’s theorem, the area of the clipped rings left of a line down the strip is a sum over their sides, of the area between each side and the strip’s top edge. Each side’s share then falls to the columns it crosses. The holes run the other way round, and take their area away. A part that covers no cell so much sews nothing, and SC-W0319 says so, as Ink/Stitch warns of it.

Dense upright crosses look at a second grid too, its lines half a cell left of and above the first grid’s, and a cell of it sews a cross by the same rule.

Every cell within the bounds costs a unit of work before any is looked at. A row of cells, held while they are looked at, and each cell that sews a cross must fit in the stitches left (REQ-GEN-004). A grid of 0.1 mm over a part 1 m wide holds 10⁸ cells, and ends with SC-E0004 before it takes memory.

Full crosses

A full cross is a stack of bars through its cell’s middle, sewn from the bottom up:

MethodBars, from the bottom upGood joinsBad joins
simple\, /the corners of /the corners of \
uprightthe bar across, the bar downthe middles of the top and bottom sidesthe middles of the left and right sides
double\, the bar across, the bar down, /the corners of /the corners of \
Smyrna\, /, the bar across, the bar downthe middles of the top and bottom sidesthe 4 corners

The bar across runs from the middle of the cell’s left side to the middle of its right side, and the bar down from the middle of its top side to the middle of its bottom side. A dense upright cross adds upright crosses on the second grid. Each has its middle on a corner of the first grid, and its arms end at the middles of the sides of the first grid’s cells, where the first grid’s arms end too.

A cross joins the run that sews it at its joins. Its good joins are the ends of its top bar. A loop from one of them goes in to the middle along the top bar, which covers that stitch later. The loop then leaves nothing on top. From a bad one, the loop ends with a stitch from the middle back to it, on top of the bars.

A loop

Each cross is sewn as a loop from one of its joins back to it. Each half of each bar takes cross_thread_count passes of the thread, the needle going back and forth along the bar, and the count is even. An odd count sews the even count below it, as in Ink/Stitch, and 1 sews 2, with SC-W0320. Ink/Stitch sews a stitch from a corner of each cell to its middle for 1, and no cross (DEV-FILL-027). With n the count’s half:

  • From an end of the top bar, the loop goes in to the middle along the top bar. It then sews the bars below the top one from the bottom up, each n times back and forth from its first end and back to the middle, and ends with the top bar n times back and forth from its far end to the start.
  • From an end of the bottom bar, the loop sews that bar back and forth from the start, ending at its far end, and goes to the middle. It sews the bars above it from the bottom up as above, and ends with the stitch from the middle to the start.
  • From an end of a bar between, a Smyrna cross’s / diagonal, the loop goes in to the middle along that bar and sews the bars from the bottom up. That bar goes from the middle to its far end and back and forth 1 time fewer, ending there. The loop ends with the stitch from the middle to the start.

A bar’s first end is the one the needle goes to first from the middle. For the \ diagonal it is the top end, or the bottom end in a loop from the bottom left corner. For the / diagonal it is the top end, but a Smyrna cross’s bottom end, unless its loop starts at the bottom right corner. The bar across starts from its left end. The bar down starts from its top end, but a Smyrna cross’s from its bottom end in a loop from the top left corner. So each loop is the one Ink/Stitch sews from that join, needle point for needle point.

Flipped crosses lie on the same grid. A flipped cross is a plain cross turned a quarter turn, each bar in the place of the bar a quarter turn from it, so that a simple cross’s / diagonal goes under its \ one and an upright cross’s bar down under its bar across. The part, its grid, the needle and the end are turned a quarter turn, sewn as plain crosses, and turned back, exactly. Ink/Stitch turns the part alone and lays its grid afresh over it. Its flipped cells are then the pattern size’s second length wide and its first tall, the offset’s second length moves them right and its first down, and with canvas_grid_origin off its grid starts at the part’s bottom left corner (DEV-FILL-026). On square cells, without an offset and on the canvas’s grid, both lay the same crosses.

Sewing the crosses

Crosses that share a join form a group: simple, double and Smyrna crosses side by side or corner to corner, upright crosses side by side, and the 2 grids’ dense upright crosses through the ends of the arms they share. Each group is one run, from a join back to it. The run starts with a cross at its join, one whose good join it is if it has one, and goes round its loop. At each place along the run, before going on, it sews the loop of each cross not sewn yet whose good join lies there. Of several, the cross lower down goes first, and of 2 level with each other the one further right, each loop sewn the same way as the run goes along it. Such a loop leaves nothing on top. Where the run has been round and crosses are left, which only their bad joins join to it, it takes the first place along it where such a cross has one, in the same order. It sews that loop there, ending on top, and goes on as before. Every join of a cross sewn lies on the run, and every cross of the group is sewn.

The groups go in this order:

  • The group with the join nearest the end is sewn last, from that join, and the part ends there. The end is the next part’s point nearest this one, or for the last part the next element’s offer, or the end position.
  • The others go first, each the group with the join nearest the needle, from that join, the needle being where the run before it ended. With no needle, the first is the group of the first cross, row by row from the top, from that cross’s first good join clockwise from its top left: a simple or double cross’s top right corner, an upright or Smyrna cross’s top middle.
  • A part of one group with both a needle and an end first runs from its join nearest the needle to its join nearest the end through the middles of the fewest crosses. Its loops start from there, and the crosses sewn later cover these stitches.

Each run is a group of stitches of its own, which assembly joins to the next with a stitch when it starts within the element’s jump length, and with a jump otherwise, as Ink/Stitch’s stitch plan joins its runs. The parts are sewn nearest the needle first, as a tatami fill’s are, and a fill with a start position starts its first part nearest it and each part after nearest where the part before ended (DEV-FILL-023).

Half crosses

A half cross sews one diagonal in each cell, \, or / for half_cross_flipped, on the same grid. The needle’s way from cell to cell lies bare. So the diagonals that run on from cell to cell are joined into rows, which are routed as a tatami fill’s rows, as Ink/Stitch routes its half crosses (read at d59c9ab, lib/stitches/cross_stitch_half.py):

  1. Pieces. Cells that share a side or a corner are one piece, and each piece is sewn on its own. The pieces nearest the needle go first, as a fill’s parts do, each ending at its point nearest the next.
  2. Outline. A piece’s rings are the sides of its cells that no other cell of it shares, joined end to end, the piece on their left as a fill’s rings have it. Where 2 cells of the piece meet only at a corner, the ring runs through the corner from the one to the other, as Ink/Stitch’s union of the cells, grown by a hundred-thousandth of a CSS pixel, joins them. Each ring starts at its top left corner.
  3. Rows. Each cell’s diagonal is split into the fewest equal stitches no longer than max_cross_stitch_length_mm, and diagonals that run on from cell to cell are one row. A \ row runs from its top end and a / row from its bottom end, the rows in the order of their first cells, row by row from the top.
  4. Route. The rows are routed as a tatami fill’s rows with underpath off (REQ-FILL-TAT-001), each sewn once. Between them the needle is sent along the rings in running stitches of max_cross_stitch_length_mm, within 0.2 CSS pixels of the way, as in Ink/Stitch’s half crosses. Those stitches lie on the outline of the cells, where nothing covers them.
  5. Passes. Each stitch of a row is sewn there and back again until it lies an odd number of times, as bean stitch sews it, and the travel lies once. Ink/Stitch takes half of cross_thread_count as the bean stitch’s repeats. So an odd count is the number of times, and an even count sews the odd count above it, 4 sewing 5, with SC-W0322.

The tatami fill’s route differs from Ink/Stitch’s where its own ledger entries say (DEV-FILL-005, DEV-FILL-006, DEV-FILL-011). Ink/Stitch’s grown union rounds each outer corner of the outline by its hundred-thousandth of a pixel. Its way between rows then cuts such a corner by up to its tolerance, 0.05 mm. Where no row joins a hole’s ring to another ring, Ink/Stitch would leave out the rows its walk finds no way to, where StitchCraft’s needle goes to them along the rows.

Stitches

Each place of a run is a needle point. A stitch longer than max_cross_stitch_length_mm is split into the fewest equal stitches no longer, as Ink/Stitch’s geometry library splits it, and a stitch exactly as long is not split. A longest stitch below twice the shortest stitch is raised to it, with SC-W0402, as a fill’s is, and every piece of a split stitch is at least the shortest stitch. A needle point nearer the one before than the shortest stitch is left out, as finalize would leave it out. On a grid so fine that the method’s shortest stitches are shorter than the shortest stitch, SC-W0321 says so:

  • a simple cross’s run from a corner to its middle, half its diagonal,
  • an upright, dense upright, double or Smyrna cross’s run from the middle of a side to its middle, half the cell’s shorter side,
  • a half cross’s stitches between rows, each a side of a cell.

A cross stitch fill sews its crosses alone, without the underlay, pull compensation or random stitch lengths of a tatami fill, as in Ink/Stitch. A preview too costly to sew shows each cross’s bars, or each half cross’s diagonal.

How it compares with Ink/Stitch

The crosses, their loops and the stitches of each are the same in both, and the measures of the differential agree. These differ:

  • Orders. Ink/Stitch takes its groups, and the crosses that meet at a place, in the order of a Python set of its objects, which follows where they lie in memory, where StitchCraft’s order is fixed. Where the needle and the end lie nearest one group of several, it sews another group first, from that group’s join nearest the end. And with neither, it starts each group at the first good join it finds of one cross alone (DEV-FILL-028). Of parts that reach equally far left, StitchCraft sews first the one whose leftmost point lies highest. Ink/Stitch sews first the one shapely gives first once it has repaired and grown the shape, which can be the lower (DEV-FILL-029).
  • Cells covered exactly half. On a file whose viewBox is in millimetres, Ink/Stitch writes the drawing’s scale with 6 decimals, 3.779528 pixels to the millimetre, against 3.7795275… for its grid. Its drawing is then a ten-millionth larger than its grid, about the page’s origin. A cell that a part covers exactly half is then covered less than the slack allows on the part’s left and top sides, some 13 mm or more from the origin, and more on the right and bottom sides. So a dense upright square drawn on the grid loses the second grid’s crosses along its left and top sides in Ink/Stitch, and keeps those along its right and bottom sides. StitchCraft reads the drawing exactly, and sews all 4 sides. Grown by 0.1 mm, as Ink/Stitch’s fonts grow their letters, the cells along every side are covered more than half, and both sew them.

Test sheet

TS-17A sews squares 24 mm wide on grids of 2 and 3 mm, with 2 and 4 threads, plain and flipped, and a K whose slanted edges cut their cells in half, with the fonts’ 2 threads and 0.1 mm of growth. A third row sews upright, dense upright, double and Smyrna crosses on the 3 mm grid, and a fourth half crosses, plain and flipped, and a square turned 30° with its grid (machine testing). Every sample but the turned one lies on the canvas’s grid, its corner a multiple of 6 mm from the origin, so that the squares sew whole cells. VectorCraft’s Test Sheet filter moves the sheet by whole multiples of 6 mm, which keep them, and the turned square’s grid starts at its own corner. The differential compares the sheet with Ink/Stitch’s sewing of it (REQ-FILL-CRS-007, REQ-FILL-CRS-012), and the case inkstitch-cross-methods adds each method’s flipped variant, turned grids, and half crosses in pieces, round a hole and through a corner.
