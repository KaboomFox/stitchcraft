Linear gradient fill

Rows of running stitches across the fill, each in one of the colours of a linear gradient (P3, M10.12). Thread can’t mix colours, and the fill blends them by sharing out rows instead. Between 2 stops of the gradient, the first stop’s colour takes most rows near it and the second’s most rows near the second stop, in a fixed pattern. Seen from a step away, the 2 colours blend. Each colour’s rows sew in that colour’s thread.

StitchCraft sews Ink/Stitch’s linear gradient fill as Ink/Stitch sews it, read at d59c9ab: lib/stitches/linear_gradient_fill.py for the rows and their colours, and lib/elements/fill_stitch.py for the fill element’s gradient, its underlay and its order of colours. What follows puts that behaviour in our own words (ADR-0012), and the generator is written from these words. Ink/Stitch was also run as a black box on gradients made for the purpose, with the cases below.

Parameters

A gradient fill has one setting of its own, stop_at_ending_point, which it shares with the tartan fill. It reads the tatami fill’s row_spacing_mm, max_stitch_length_mm, staggers, skip_last and expand_mm, its whole underlay, and angle, which turns the rows of a fill without a gradient and that fill’s underlay. It reads the running stitch’s length and tolerance for the needle’s path along the outline, its random stitch lengths and random_seed. Ink/Stitch’s gradient fill takes no pull compensation, end row spacing, gap-filling rows or underpath, and StitchCraft’s doesn’t either.

The gradient

A linear gradient fill sews the gradient its shape is painted with. The gradient has a start and an end in the design’s millimetres, every transform applied, and stops (data model). The SVG and VectorCraft adapters place it (SVG input, VectorCraft integration). Each stop has an offset from 0 at the start to 1 at the end, a colour and an opacity. A stop of opacity 0 is transparent, and the rows it would take are left out. Any other opacity sews its colour.

A fill without a linear gradient sews its own colour fading out, as below. One painted with a plain colour is such a fill. SC-W0329 says so, as Ink/Stitch warns of it.

Rows

The fill’s parts are grown by expand_mm and cut into pieces as a tatami fill’s parts are (fills). Each piece gets rows of its own.

The way along the gradient points from its start to its end. Where the 2 are one point, the way is along x, to the right. The rows lie square to it. Each runs the way along the gradient turned a quarter turn clockwise, as the canvas shows it with y down: down the canvas when the gradient points right.

Where the rows lie. Measured along the gradient, the piece’s outline lies between its nearest point and its farthest. The rows start a longest stitch before the nearest and end a longest stitch past the farthest. That length is cut into equal steps no longer than the row spacing, and a row lies at each end of each step: row 0 at the near end, and the last row at the far end. Measured across the gradient, each row starts a row spacing before the outline and ends a row spacing past it.

Needle points. Along each row, from its first end, row k’s needle points lie the longest stitch apart. The first lies k ÷ staggers of a stitch on, less its whole stitches. Each row’s points then lie a staggers-th of a stitch on from the row before’s. A staggers of 0 counts as 1. The points stop short of the row’s last end. A row’s first end is a needle point too when the first point lies more than half a row spacing from it, and its last end when the last point does. A row with room for fewer than 2 such points is its 2 ends. With enable_random_stitch_length, a row is a running stitch from its first end to its last at random lengths, each row drawing from a stream of the element’s generator that its number keys. Ink/Stitch seeds each row’s numbers itself, and they differ (DEV-FILL-004).

Each stop’s row

Each stop lies at a row. The gradient’s start lies at row s, the row spacings from row 0 to the start along the gradient, no more than the rows’ whole length, rounded to the nearest whole number, halves to the even one. Where that gives 0, s is minus the row spacings from the gradient’s start to row 0, no more than the gradient’s length, rounded the same way. The steps between rows can be a little shorter than the row spacing. Ink/Stitch counts in row spacings all the same, and so does StitchCraft. A stop at offset t lies at row s plus the gradient’s length times t, in row spacings and rounded.

A fill without a gradient sews 2 stops: its own colour at row 0, and a transparent one a row past the last. Its rows lie square to the way at angle, turned clockwise as the canvas shows it: at 30° the way points right and 30° down. The colour fades out along it.

The colours of the rows

The stops are taken in order, each with its row, and the rows go to their colours:

  1. The first stop takes rows 0 to its own row, when its row is past row 0.
  2. Each stop after it blends with the colour before it. The blend starts at the row after the last blend, or after the first stop’s row, and its length is the rows from there to the stop’s row. Where the stop’s row and the row before its blend both lie before row 0, the stop blends nothing. The next stop then blends from it.
  3. A blend of 2 rows or fewer is no blend. The stop is passed over, and the next blend starts a row further on. That leaves a row out, and the next stop blends from the colour before the passed one. SC-W0330 says so where the row left out crosses the piece. A gradient whose start and end are one point puts every stop at one row, and all but its first are passed over.
  4. After the last blend, every row left takes the last stop’s colour.

A blend lays its rows in a fixed pattern, and it can end a row or 2 before the stop’s row, or past it. The next blend starts where it ended. The blends never give row 0 to a colour, which only the first stop’s rows or those after the last blend can take. A row past the last is left out, and so are the rows of a transparent stop. Stops of one colour share their rows.

A blend’s pattern. Say the blend is T rows long, and n is the whole square root of T. Its first half is laid one row after the other, in rounds 1 to n:

  • Round i has i runs, each some rows of the first colour and then one row of the second. The round shares n − i rows of the first colour among its runs as evenly as they go, its first runs taking the rows left over.
  • Half the rows past n², rounded down, go one each to the first runs laid, a row of the first colour more each.
  • No run has more rows of the first colour than the last run of the round before.
  • The half ends at the first row of the first colour laid 2 rows short of half the blend or later. One row of the second colour follows it and ends the half.

The second half is the first half backwards, each row in the other colour. The first colour’s runs shorten towards the middle, and the second colour’s lengthen after it. A blend of 12 rows, 1 for the first colour and 2 for the second, is 1 1 1 2 1 2 1 2 1 2 2 2.

Their order. Each colour lists its rows as they come. The first stop’s rows come from row 0. A blend’s rows of a colour follow, its first half’s in their order and its second half’s from its end back to the middle. The rows after the last blend come last. Each colour’s rows are routed in that order.

Sewing

A gradient sews its parts in a tatami fill’s order, nearest the needle first (fills). Each part sews the tatami fill’s underlay first, in the colour of the gradient’s first stop, transparent or not. Its rows run along the gradient, square to the top rows, unless fill_underlay_angle sets their angles. A fill without a gradient sews its underlay as a tatami fill’s, square to its angle.

The colours of each piece come in the order the stops first give them, the transparent ones left out. The outline cuts each colour’s rows, and each piece of row keeps the needle points inside it. A colour’s pieces of row are routed as a tatami fill’s rows are routed (routing) and sewn once. Between them the needle follows the piece’s outline, never under the rows. The routes of a piece all start where the needle was when its colours began, after its underlay, and head for the end the next part or element offers. A colour that leaves the piece’s rows out sews nothing.

Ends left out. The stitches before a colour’s first needle point on a row are left out, unless the colour is the piece’s first and the fill has an underlay: then it runs on from the underlay. The stitches after its last such point are left out too. With stop_at_ending_point on, the last 2 colours keep them, as Ink/Stitch counts them. A colour with no needle point on a row before its last point keeps the stitches before it. One with none after its first keeps all but its last point, as Ink/Stitch strips them.

Several parts. Each part sews its underlay and its colours. As Ink/Stitch sorts a gradient’s groups, the underlays and colours are then ordered by where their colour first comes among them. Each thread then sews in one stretch.

Threads and colour blocks. Each colour of each piece is a colour block of its own thread, which assembly trims after when the element’s trim_after is on. The underlay ends no colour block (several threads in one element). A colour of the element’s own thread sews in it, named or not, and the others in threads of their colours alone, as a tartan’s do. The sewing order reads a gradient’s threads from its paint and settings alone, before it is sewn. The first stop’s colour comes first when the fill has an underlay, then the colours of the stops that aren’t transparent, in their order.

Stitches. A needle point nearer the one before than the element’s shortest stitch is left out, as finalize would leave it out (DEV-FIN-001). The first colour sews on from the underlay in one thread. Its run starts a shortest stitch from where the underlay ended, as does any run that sews on from the one before in its thread. The budget is charged for the rows and their needle points, the cuts, the colours’ rows and the stitches, before memory is taken for them.

A preview too costly to sew shows each colour’s rows inside the pieces, without the stitches.

How it compares with Ink/Stitch

The differential sews TS-17B’s gradients and 6 files of its own in both programs (REQ-FILL-GRD-009). Their colour blocks are the same, in the same threads, with the same trims and jumps, but where the ledger explains otherwise. Where the counts of stitches agree within the differential’s 10 %, StitchCraft sews 0.4 to 8 % fewer than Ink/Stitch in the differential’s own files, and 9 % fewer over the whole of TS-17B, whose tartans count too. StitchCraft differs where the drawing and Ink/Stitch’s sewing part:

  • Ink/Stitch works out the underlay’s angle from the gradient before it applies the gradient’s gradientTransform. The underlay of a turned gradient then doesn’t lie along it. StitchCraft’s lies along the gradient as the file draws it (DEV-FILL-031).
  • Where every stop’s row lies before row 0, Ink/Stitch counts the rows the last blend’s end lies before row 0. It sews that many rows from the far end back in the last colour and leaves the rest unsewn. StitchCraft sews every row after the last blend in the last stop’s colour, as a viewer paints the fill past its last stop (DEV-FILL-032).

The SVG adapter places a gradient as a viewer does, which Ink/Stitch doesn’t for 2 kinds of file (SVG input). Ink/Stitch counts the rows’ steps in pixels. Under a gradient across a square 40 mm a side, the rows span 46 mm, a whole number of row spacings. There Ink/Stitch’s rounding can give one step more, and its rows lie a little closer. TS-17B’s gradients slant, which keeps them off that edge.

Test sheet

TS-17B’s third row sews linear gradients in squares 40 mm a side, each trimmed after each colour (machine testing). From left to right, red blends into blue up to the right and deep gold through white into emerald green down to the right. The last blends tangerine into Prussian blue down to the left, through a transparent white. Every stop’s colour is one of Brother’s threads, so both programs’ files name each thread as itself. Each gradient’s own thread is its first stop’s colour, unnamed, as the stops’ threads are.
