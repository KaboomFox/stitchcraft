StitchCraft

Fill generators

A fill covers the area its path bounds, its region, with rows of stitches. Tatami is P1 (M5); contour, meander and circular are P2 (M7); guided, linear gradient, tartan and cross stitch are P3 (M10). Ink/Stitch’s legacy fill is not planned, and is sewn as a tatami fill (below).

A fill sews the drawing’s shape with Ink/Stitch’s stitches. Ink/Stitch’s rows, their spacing and stagger, the order it sews them in, its travel, underlay and compensation are what make its fills look good embroidered. StitchCraft keeps them, with their parameters’ names and defaults, and a file sews alike in both. Where the area Ink/Stitch fills departs from the drawing, StitchCraft follows the drawing, and a diagnostic says where the two differ. A part Ink/Stitch leaves out without a word is left out with a warning.

A fill whose fill setting is off sews nothing, and nothing is said of it, as in Ink/Stitch (REQ-FILL-003). The path’s outline, if it has one, is sewn as its stroke settings say, and the element before it still ends near its area, as Ink/Stitch’s does.

Region

A fill’s region is the area an SVG renderer paints for its path, in parts with holes (stitchcraft_engine::normalize::region, REQ-FILL-001):

  1. Rings. Each subpath is flattened within a tenth of a CSS pixel, as Ink/Stitch flattens a fill’s outline, and closed. A subpath of fewer than 3 distinct points bounds nothing and is left out (DEV-FILL-002).
  2. Cuts. Wherever 2 segments cross, touch or run along each other, both are cut there, on a grid of 2⁻³⁵ mm, about 2.9 × 10⁻¹¹ mm. The rings’ points go on the grid, much as Ink/Stitch puts a fill’s shape on a grid of 10⁻¹⁰ CSS pixels, about 2.6 × 10⁻¹¹ mm. So the corners that shapes share, which relative path data and transforms leave a few units in the last place apart, become one point, and a gap or an overlap that narrow closes. A segment that passes through the cell of a corner, or of a point where 2 segments cross, is bent through that point. This is snap rounding, and the edges then meet only at their ends however rounding left the drawing. Before it, crossings worked out in floating point and snapped to points nearby could leave 2 edges crossing with no point between them, and whole faces filled or empty against the rule. On the grid, which side of a line a point lies on and which way a ring turns are decided exactly. A stretch drawn more than once becomes one edge that counts how many times it was drawn each way.
  3. Faces. The edges bound faces. Outside everything the winding number is 0, and crossing an edge from its right to its left adds its count. Every face’s winding number follows from its neighbours’ in whole numbers.
  4. Fill rule. A face is filled when its winding number is not 0 (nonzero, SVG’s default) or is odd (evenodd). Ink/Stitch’s area differs in 2 ways, and StitchCraft follows the drawing in both (DEV-FILL-001). Ink/Stitch takes every subpath but the largest as a hole. Where nonzero fills a face inside that even-odd leaves empty, StitchCraft fills it, and SC-I0306 says Ink/Stitch leaves it empty. And Ink/Stitch repairs a shape that is not valid by merging the subpaths’ lines, a stretch drawn twice into one, and filling the faces they bound by how deeply they nest. A face beside the outside nests in nothing, and is always filled. Where the fill rule leaves such a face empty, a notch drawn as a hole along the outline or a subpath drawn twice, SC-I0313 says Ink/Stitch fills it.
  5. Rings of the parts. The edges between filled and empty faces are joined into rings with the filled side on their right. Outlines turn clockwise and holes counter-clockwise in y-up axes, the other way round on screen, where y points down. Where rings meet at a point, they are joined the way GEOS joins them, the geometry library behind Ink/Stitch’s shapes. Parts that touch at a point stay apart. A hole that touches its outline stays a hole. Each ring starts at its point that comes first in the drawing, and parts and holes keep the drawing’s order too.
  6. Parts too small to sew. A part of 3 square CSS pixels or less (0.21 mm²) is too small for a row of stitches and is left out. Ink/Stitch leaves it out without a word. StitchCraft says so with SC-W0303 (REQ-FILL-002). A fill under 20 square CSS pixels (1.4 mm²) gets SC-W0304, as Ink/Stitch warns of it. A fill in parts gets SC-W0307, since each part is sewn on its own.

The work is charged to the element’s budget, one unit for each point, edge bent and grid point tested against it, pair of edges whose spans along x overlap, half edge, ring compared with a face or an outline, and point walked to find a ring’s bounds or its place among the faces and outlines (REQ-FILL-004). Each ring’s bounds are found once. A ring that bounds no face is located only in the faces whose bounds cover its own, and a hole apart from every outline only in the outlines whose bounds cover its own. Property tests draw random rings on a grid, and random rings with float coordinates beside near-copies of themselves, moved or turned by a hair or drawn back, and count how many times they wind round points off the edges. Such a point lies in the region exactly when the fill rule fills it, unless it lies in a part left out. Every ring found is simple and turns the right way, and the edges the rings are cut into meet only at their ends. Drawings of shapes that share corners and edges, written with relative path data as Inkscape writes them, fill their shapes’ area, in 1 part where the shapes share edges.

Regions are combined by the same cuts and faces (stitchcraft_engine::normalize::region::ops), as automatic digitizing combines the shapes of a picture where they meet (Neighbours). A region’s parts never overlap and its outlines turn clockwise. Its rings then wind -1 times round each point inside it and 0 times round every other point. Of the faces that the rings of 2 regions bound, the union keeps those some ring winds round and the intersection those wound -2 times. The difference first reverses the second region’s rings, then keeps the faces wound -1 times. A region grows or shrinks by each part’s offset curves, with round corners (normalize::offset), and the parts grown are joined, since they may then overlap.

Tatami fill

Parallel rows of running stitches across the region, with the needle points of neighbouring rows offset so they never line up into visible furrows. Most fills in most designs are tatami. Each step below sews as Ink/Stitch sews, on StitchCraft’s region, unless it says where it differs. The roadmap’s M5 steps build them in this order.

Parameters: angle, row_spacing_mm, end_row_spacing_mm, max_stitch_length_mm, staggers, skip_last, underpath, running_stitch_length_mm, running_stitch_tolerance_mm, gap_fill_rows, pull_compensation_mm, pull_compensation_percent, expand_mm, random_seed, enable_random_stitch_length, random_stitch_length_jitter_percent; underlay: fill_underlay, fill_underlay_angle, fill_underlay_row_spacing_mm, fill_underlay_max_stitch_length_mm, fill_underlay_inset_mm, fill_underlay_skip_last, underlay_underpath.

1. Rows

Rows are laid across each part of the region on its own (stitchcraft_engine::generators::tatami::rows, since M5.2). They run at angle, counter-clockwise from horizontal on screen: at 0° left to right, at 90° up the screen. Measured across them from the design’s origin, the first row of a part lies the largest whole number of row_spacing_mm from the origin that does not pass the part, and the rows follow it row_spacing_mm apart up to the part’s far side, with none on it (REQ-FILL-TAT-002). Fills side by side at the same angle and spacing then share their rows. Angles of whole right angles are exact, so rows at 0° or 90° run exactly along the drawing’s axes. The nth row lies n spacings past the first, multiplied out, since a spacing such as 0.1 mm rounds in binary, and added up row by row its rounding grew into a row on the far side.

With end_row_spacing_mm, each step to the next row is the row spacing plus the difference to the end spacing times the distance from the first row over the part’s height. The spacing then reaches the end spacing a part’s height from the first row. Ink/Stitch carries the change on beyond that. Over a part only a few rows tall, a shrinking spacing then turns so small that the rows pile up short of the far side without end. StitchCraft keeps the end spacing from there on (DEV-FILL-003).

A row’s segments are the stretches of it in the part, its outline included, split at every point where the row meets the outline (REQ-FILL-TAT-010). Those are the points where it crosses the outline or the outline touches it from inside, and both ends of a stretch where it runs along the outline. A row along an edge is then a segment of its own, and a row that only touches the part at a point has none. This is how GEOS, the library behind Ink/Stitch’s shapes, cuts a line with a polygon. Its answers for 2,511 rows across 406 random polygons of grid squares and half squares, 98 of them with holes, are recorded and checked (conformance/fixtures/geometry/shapely-rows.txt). Which stretches lie inside is decided by counting the edges that cross the row, each over its height from its lower end up to but not including its upper end.

A side drawn on a row lies a hair off it by the time it is cut: the region puts its corners on a grid 2.9e-11 mm wide, and the row’s place rounds. So a point of the outline within 1e-9 mm of a row lies on it, and a row within 1e-9 mm of a part’s far side lies on that side, and is left out. A part’s first and last rows, and the segments of a row drawn along a side, then don’t depend on the rounding. Ink/Stitch’s do (DEV-FILL-012).

The top rows alone, laid over each part grown by expand_mm as the fill grows it, are what a preview shows of a fill too costly to sew within the preview’s budget (generate::rows, since M6.3). Laying them costs a small part of sewing the fill, since no needle point is placed and no route is searched. A 28 mm square takes 626 units of the engine’s work for its rows, and 62,532 to sew.

2. Needle points along a row

A row’s needle points lie on a grid along it, as Ink/Stitch places them (stitchcraft_engine::generators::tatami::stitches, since M5.3). The grid’s points are the longest stitch apart, max_stitch_length_mm. A fill always has one: 4 mm when it is not set or left empty, and 0.1 mm, with SC-W0102, when it is 0 or less, as in Ink/Stitch. Measured along the rows from the design’s origin, they lie at whole numbers of stitches plus the row’s offset (REQ-FILL-TAT-003). A row’s number is how far across the rows it lies from the origin, over row_spacing_mm, rounded to the nearest whole number, halves to the even one. Its offset is the fractional part of that number over staggers, times the longest stitch. Each row’s points then lie a staggers-th of a stitch along from the row before’s and come back after staggers rows, so neighbouring rows never line them up into furrows. A fraction of a stagger draws diagonals that show less. A negative staggers staggers the rows the other way, and 0 counts as 1, which lines every row’s points up, as in Ink/Stitch. Fills side by side at the same angle share the grid, as they share their rows.

A segment is sewn from one end to the other, as routing decides. It takes its start, then every grid point past the start and before the end, and then its end, unless skip_last is on or the last point lies within 0.1 mm of it. A start on the grid is sewn once. Sewn the other way, a segment takes the same grid points.

With enable_random_stitch_length, random lengths take the grid’s place. The point nearest the start lies a random share of the longest stitch past it, and each next one a stitch on, longer or shorter by up to random_stitch_length_jitter_percent of it, while they lie before the end. The draws come from the element’s generator, seeded with random_seed, in the order the segments are sewn. Ink/Stitch seeds a generator for each segment, and its random values differ (DEV-FILL-004).

3. Routing: every segment once

Each part’s segments are sewn once each, in the order Ink/Stitch routes them (stitchcraft_engine::generators::tatami::route, since M5.4, REQ-FILL-TAT-001).

  1. Order. The segments are taken row by row, across the rows, and within a row by how far their start lies from the corner of the part’s bounding box where x and y are least. Each runs along its row.
  2. Graph. Each segment is an edge between its 2 ends, and the ends are nodes. A node belongs to the ring nearest it, or to the outline when a hole is as near. Its place is its distance along that ring from the ring’s start. Along each ring an edge joins each node to the next, and every other one of those stretches, from the first, gets a second edge. A node then meets its row and 3 stretches of ring, an even number of edges. Ink/Stitch’s graph knows an edge between 2 nodes by its kind, so a ring of 2 nodes doubles only one of its 2 stretches. This graph does the same.
  3. Start and end. The fill starts at the point of its rings nearest the needle and ends at the point nearest its next stitch, which the next element’s shape gives (section 5). Each of the 2 points gets a node, which splits the stretch of that ring whose chord passes nearest it. Then the node nearest the needle is where the fill starts, and the one nearest the next stitch where it ends. Without a needle the fill starts at its first segment’s start, and without a next element it ends where it starts. Only rings that rows reach are looked at. Ink/Stitch joins an unreached ring with straight lines, which it sews as rows (DEV-FILL-006).
  4. Even degrees. A row through a corner of the outline, or one along it, shares a node with the row beside it, and some nodes then meet an odd number of edges. Ink/Stitch adds paths between such nodes that may run along rows, and sews those rows a second time. StitchCraft pairs the odd nodes so that the needle’s way between pairs is shortest in all and joins each pair with an edge. Up to 16 nodes every pairing is weighed, and past that each node pairs with the nearest left. No row is sewn twice (DEV-FILL-006). A pairing edge stands for the way between its 2 nodes that Ink/Stitch’s paths double: one edge where a row or a stretch of ring joins the 2, and several otherwise. Travel along it is never a handover between neighbouring rows for skip_last, and it ends a stretch for gap-fill rows only where it stands for several.
  5. Walk. Ink/Stitch walks a copy of the graph, which its graph library makes by adding the edges again, node by node in the order the nodes came, each node’s edges in its own order. So in the copy a node lists first its neighbours that came before it, in the order they came, and then the others, itself included, in its own order. The walk starts at the end node. At each node it takes the node’s row if one is left, and otherwise the node’s first edge left. A node lists its edges neighbour by neighbour, each neighbour’s in the order they came. A neighbour whose last edge is taken drops out of the list. When the walk reaches a node with no edge left it steps back, and the edges, in the order it steps back over them, are the route. The route comes back to the end node, and before it the needle runs from the fill’s start to that node. The running stitches between rows then mostly come just before the rows beside them, which cover them, and the rows go back and forth like a mown lawn.
  6. Between rows. Steps between rows in a row are one stretch, from where the last row ended to where the next starts. The needle travels it under the rows (section 4), or with underpath off along the ring between 2 points of one ring, the shorter way round, and between 2 rings the shortest way along rings and rows (DEV-FILL-006). Ink/Stitch’s needle takes the way round with fewer nodes of its graph, a node every CSS pixel along the outline’s sides, which is about the shorter way but not always (DEV-FILL-011). It sews running stitches of the first running_stitch_length_mm, at least twice the shortest stitch, within running_stitch_tolerance_mm. Ink/Stitch reads a fill’s value as one number, and sews a value of several in its default, 2.5 mm (DEV-SAT-005). The first running stitch would sew the row’s end again, so it is left out. With skip_last the row’s end is not sewn, and the first running stitch stays unless the next row starts beside it.
  7. The shortest stitch. Rows the row spacing apart start and end nearer each other than the shortest stitch a machine sews well, and a row’s first grid point can lie just past its start. A needle point nearer the one before it than the shortest stitch is left out, as Ink/Stitch leaves out points nearer than its own shortest stitch. A part’s last point stays, and the points before it that are too near it go instead, unless that would bring it within the shortest stitch of the part’s first: then it goes itself. This is finalize’s rule, applied in the generator. Where the rows lie nearer each other than the machine’s shortest stitch and meet an edge square on, each row hands over to the next in a stitch too short to sew, so the next row’s first needle point is left out (DEV-FIN-001). The thread then runs from the row’s end to the next row’s second needle point, and a sliver at each row’s start shows. On the Brother, whose shortest stitch is 0.3 mm, a bar 5 mm wide at Ink/Stitch’s default of 0.25 mm is 97 % covered, and at 0.3 mm or more it is covered to its edges (REQ-FILL-TAT-004). Ink/Stitch’s shortest stitch, 0.1 mm by default, keeps the handover. Along an edge at a slant to the rows, their ends lie farther apart: the spacing over the sine of the angle between edge and rows, 0.35 mm at 45° for rows 0.25 mm apart, which the Brother sews.

The route follows Ink/Stitch’s from the same graph. StitchCraft’s rings start where the drawing starts them, and Ink/Stitch’s where GEOS’s overlay starts them, so which stretches are doubled, and with them the route, can differ (DEV-FILL-005).

4. Travel under the rows

With underpath, on by default, the needle runs between rows inside the part, under the rows sewn after it, which cover it, as Ink/Stitch’s does (stitchcraft_engine::generators::tatami::underpath, since M5.5, REQ-FILL-TAT-005).

  1. Lines. 3 gratings of lines cross the part, laid as rows are (section 1). 2 of them lie at 45° to the rows, one either way, their lines 2 mm apart, and the third square to the rows, its lines √2 mm apart. A part smaller than 10,000 square CSS pixels (700 mm²) has less room, and its lines lie half as far apart. The square lines pass through the crossings of the others, so the 3 make one network. Its nodes are the crossings, the lines’ ends on the rings and the route’s nodes, and along each ring an edge joins each node to the next. A crossing within 0.005 px of a node already found is that node. Where either 45° grating misses the part, the needle runs as with underpath off. Ink/Stitch smooths that way as it smooths a way under the rows, step 4, and StitchCraft keeps to the rings (DEV-FILL-013).
  2. Costs. A piece of line costs its length in CSS pixels over its distance from the part’s rings, simplified within 0.5 mm, plus a tenth of a pixel. A stretch of ring costs 3 times the straight line between its ends. Travel then keeps to the middle of the part, away from its edges.
  3. Sewn rows close lines. Once a row is sewn, the pieces of line that cross it close, and no later travel follows them over it.
  4. The way. The needle takes the cheapest way. The way is cut into points 10 px apart within 4 px of it, the corners of the points after its start are cut 5 times, a quarter of the way along each side from each end, and its own ends go back at either end: it leaves its start straight and curves from there. Then it is kept inside the part. Where it leaves the part or runs along a ring, the ring takes its place, the shorter way round, from where it leaves to where it comes back: the hole it leaves into, or else the outline. It is sewn in running stitches as in section 3.
  5. A hole between the lines. Where no line joins the 2 points, as for a hole small enough to lie between the lines, the needle runs along the rings and rows, as with underpath off. Ink/Stitch goes straight there, but it travels between 2 rings only from the fill’s start to its end, before any row is sewn. StitchCraft also travels between rings to pair nodes (DEV-FILL-006), after rows are sewn, which a straight way would cross.
  6. A needle inside the part. The fill starts at the node of the network nearest the needle (since M5.7). For a needle on the rings or outside the part, that is the route’s start. For one inside, as after an inset underlay, it is a node inside, and the needle travels the cheapest way from there to the route’s start, or on to the end of the route’s first stretch of travel, as Ink/Stitch’s does.

Equally cheap ways, the simplified rings and the points a way is cut into follow StitchCraft’s own rules, so the way can differ from Ink/Stitch’s in its details (DEV-FILL-007).

Travel asks the rings where a point lies on them, how far a line lies from them, and where a stitch meets them or lies inside. Each question looks only at the ring sides near its point, line or stitch, filed in a grid of square cells about as many as the sides, and gets the answer a look at every side would give (REQ-FILL-TAT-013).

5. Parts

A region in parts sews them one at a time, as Ink/Stitch sews them (REQ-FILL-TAT-008, since M5.4). The part nearest the needle goes first, a part the needle is in being 0 away. With no needle, the part with the least x goes first. Ink/Stitch sorts the parts with a stable sort. GEOS’s repair gave the parts of 400 of 400 random fills tied at their least x in order of their point of least x, and of least y among the points level with it (shapely 2.2.0, GEOS 3.14.1). Parts that tie go in that order here too. Each part ends at its point nearest the next part. The last ends at the point of the fill nearest its next stitch. That is the next element’s first stitch when it lies in the fill. Of a satin column’s rails, the start of the first that lies in the fill is the next stitch, and of a fill’s parts, the first outline’s start that does. Failing those, for a fill after a fill, it is the first start of this fill’s outlines that lies in the next, and otherwise the fill’s point nearest the next element. Ink/Stitch’s geometry library finds it so (satin). Each part is a group of its own, which assembly joins to the next as it joins any 2 groups: straight on within the collapse length, and otherwise with a jump and the ties and trims the element’s settings give. SC-W0307 says the fill is in parts.

6. Underlay

Before its top rows, each part sews its underlay, on by default (REQ-FILL-TAT-011, since M5.6). The underlay is a layer of rows for each angle in fill_underlay_angle, in the order given, or with none set one at angle + 90°, square to the top rows. A layer’s rows lie evenly fill_underlay_row_spacing_mm apart (default: 3 times the row spacing), with no end spacing. Their needle points lie on the stagger grid with the fill’s staggers, fill_underlay_max_stitch_length_mm apart (default: the fill’s longest stitch), never at random lengths. fill_underlay_skip_last and underlay_underpath take the place of skip_last and underpath.

Each layer is routed as the top rows are (sections 3 and 4). It starts at the point of the part’s rings nearest the needle and ends there, as Ink/Stitch routes a fill with nothing to end near. The next layer, and then the top rows, start where it ended, so a part is still one run. A layer with no row is sewn round the part’s outline from its start, as the top rows are (section 8), and SC-I0308 says so. The needle goes on to that start as Ink/Stitch’s stitch plan joins a layer to the next one: straight on within the element’s jump length, and farther with a jump. The generator makes that join itself, because Ink/Stitch never forces locks after a layer of underlay, where assembly would after any group of an element with force_lock_stitches.

The underlay is sewn on the part shrunk by fill_underlay_inset_mm, and the top rows on the part grown by expand_mm, or shrunk where it is negative (REQ-FILL-TAT-012, since M5.7). A part is grown or shrunk as GEOS buffers a polygon, which Ink/Stitch calls, with round corners of 16 segments to a quarter circle (stitchcraft_engine::normalize::offset). Each ring is offset, the offsets are cut where they cross, and the faces they wind round clockwise at least once are the area. Before offsetting, GEOS leaves out a ring that shrinking wears away. After, it leaves out a small ring’s offset that has turned inside out. The engine follows both rules. The polygons agree with shapely’s within a nanometre for 330 random polygons, some at the edges of both rules (conformance/oracle/buffer.py).

Shrinking can cut a part into pieces. Each piece is sewn on its own, in order of its point of least x, and of least y among pieces level with each other: a run of pieces, each with its least x within a nanometre of the one before. That is GEOS’s order in all but a few cases, where GEOS orders pieces with a sliver among them, or pieces level at their least x, as it walks its graph (DEV-FILL-008). Every layer of the underlay is sewn on one piece before the next, each starting nearest the needle. Each piece’s top rows start nearest the needle too, after the piece before, and end nearest the next part or element. Ink/Stitch starts every piece’s top rows nearest where the needle was before the first (DEV-FILL-008). Ink/Stitch sews each piece’s top rows as a group of its own. With force_lock_stitches, so does StitchCraft: assembly ties each off and jumps to the next, however near. Where shrinking wears a part away entirely, the part itself is sewn, as in Ink/Stitch, and SC-W0310 (inset) or SC-W0309 (expand) says so, as Ink/Stitch warns. Ink/Stitch also warns of an inset that wears a part away when the fill has no underlay to inset, and StitchCraft does not.

7. Pull compensation and gap-fill rows

The thread pulls a row in at its ends. Each top row’s segment is lengthened at both ends by pull_compensation_mm plus pull_compensation_percent of its length (REQ-FILL-TAT-006, since M5.8). The first value applies at the end the rows start from at the fill’s angle. A second value, if given, applies at the other end. Where 2 segments of a row meet end to end, because the row only touches the outline there from inside or runs along it (section 1), the row goes on in the part. Both segments end at that point as they are, so the row is sewn once through it, as through Ink/Stitch’s rebuilt part, where the row is one segment. Ink/Stitch moves a satin’s stitches with the same function, and the two settings are declared once for both (stitchcraft_engine::generators::pull). A fill reads a value below 0 as 0, as Ink/Stitch does, and SC-W0312 says so. The route is planned on the part as drawn. The needle’s ways keep their course, but each starts where the row before it ends and ends where the next row starts, both lengthened. Underlay is not compensated, as in Ink/Stitch.

Ink/Stitch rebuilds the part round the lengthened rows, each widened by half the row spacing with round ends, and smooths the outline. Its rows then reach up to half a row spacing further, and it fills holes smaller than a row spacing squared and holes the widened rows close over. Where the widened rows form separate pieces, it keeps only the largest. StitchCraft keeps the part’s holes, gaps and pieces, which the designer drew (DEV-FILL-009).

Where the route leaves a stretch of more than 3 rows for another, the fabric can open a gap along its last row before the rows past it are sewn. There, gap_fill_rows, rounded up to an even number, sews that many more rows past the last one, back and forth, and comes back to where it ended (REQ-FILL-TAT-007, since M5.8). A stretch’s rows are counted as Ink/Stitch counts them: the rows reached by travel. The route’s first row is one of them, because Ink/Stitch’s route always starts with travel, of no length where it starts on that row. Ink/Stitch’s gap fill ends a stretch where the walk goes from one row to the next by more than one edge once the loops between them are taken out, and so does this one. An edge that comes back to a node where an earlier one of those edges ended takes out the edges since, and itself, but a loop back to the row it left stays. So travel out and back before a handover to the row beside ends a stretch, and so does a pairing edge that stands for a way of several. The extra rows go on the way the stretch went, away from the last row sewn before the last one in another row. A last row sewn on through a joint is 2 segments of one row, and the way comes from the row before them, as Ink/Stitch takes it from the row the needle left for that row. Each extra row is the next row of the grid, the stretch of its segment beside the extra row before it, or the last row for the first, lengthened as the rows are. The extra rows are one strip, stop at the part’s edge, and stop where the part ends. The hop from each to the next, and the way back, are kept inside the part as travel is. Ink/Stitch repeats the last row a spacing further each time, and a repeat can run outside the part (DEV-FILL-010). Underlay has no gap-fill rows.

8. No rows

A part thinner than the row spacing has no row. It is sewn as a running stitch round its outline, from the outline’s start, as Ink/Stitch sews it, and SC-W0305 says so (since M5.4). A layer of underlay with no row is sewn so too, and SC-I0308 says so (since M5.6). Where the fill rule leaves every face empty, the fill sews nothing, and SC-W0303 says so.

Contour fill

Rings that follow the region’s outline inward, like growth rings (P2, M7.7), as Ink/Stitch sews its contour fill (read at 6555892). The outline and the holes are offset inward by row_spacing_mm, again and again, with the corners join_style gives them, round, mitred or bevelled, until nothing is left. Each ring lies inside the one it was offset from, and the rings form a tree. Where the shape narrows, a ring parts into several, and a hole’s ring joins the outline’s where they meet. clockwise sets which way the rings run. contour_strategy sews the tree:

  • Inner to outer (0). The needle goes round the outline from its point nearest the fill’s start. At the first point within 1.5 row spacings of a ring inside it, or within 2.05 if no point is that near, it goes on to that ring and sews it, and the rings inside it, the same way, back to where it entered. It then goes on round the outer ring, a row spacing past where it left. A ring that no point of the ring outside it comes within 2.05 row spacings of is not sewn. Each innermost ring is sewn the other way round, or with avoid_self_crossing every other level of rings is. smoothness_mm smooths the path, which is then kept inside the region.
  • Single spiral (1). One spiral from the outline in to the centre, each turn running from a ring to the next one inside it. Where a ring parts into several, the spiral goes on into the one whose centre lies nearest its own, and the others are not sewn.
  • Double spiral (2). The same rings, every other one on a spiral in and the rest on a spiral back out.

The path is sewn as a running stitch of max_stitch_length_mm within running_stitch_tolerance_mm, with bean stitch and repeats. Ink/Stitch leaves out the rings it does not reach without a word. StitchCraft names them (SC-W0311). Ink/Stitch’s repeats double the stitching each time, so that k repeats sew 2^(k−1) passes. Whether StitchCraft’s do too is settled with the fill.

Properties: rings lie the row spacing apart (± 5 %), and neighbouring rings never cross. Each strategy sews one unbroken path per part.

Meander fill

A path that wanders through a pattern (P2, M7.8), as Ink/Stitch sews its meander fill (read at 6555892). A tile’s lines are repeated over each part of the region, scaled by meander_scale_percent and turned by meander_angle. The lines that lie wholly inside the part make a graph of their ends. Lines with a free end, one that meets no other line, are taken away, again and again, until none is left, and the graph’s pieces are joined where they lie nearest. The path starts at the graph’s point nearest the fill’s start and ends at the one nearest the next element, or else nearest the point of the outline across from the start. It begins as the shortest way between the 2. Then, in an order the random seed sets, each of its steps is replaced by a detour of up to 7 steps through points the path has not passed, and each 2 steps in a row by one of up to 10, until no detour is found. The path never passes a point twice. It is smoothed by smoothness_mm and sewn as a running stitch of running_stitch_length_mm, or, where zigzag_spacing_mm is above 0, as a zigzag zigzag_width_mm wide. clip keeps it inside the part. Bean stitch and repeats follow, and the repeats double the stitching each time, as the contour fill’s do. Its random numbers are StitchCraft’s own, as for a tatami fill’s random lengths (DEV-FILL-004), and the path differs from Ink/Stitch’s for the same seed.

Tiles are StitchCraft’s own, generated in code (Hilbert, Peano, Truchet-style arcs, waves, pebbles…), never copied from other projects. meander_pattern takes our tile ids, and Ink/Stitch’s tile names map to the closest of ours as a recorded deviation.

Circular fill

A double spiral round a centre (P2, M7.9), as Ink/Stitch sews its circular fill (read at 6555892): the point of a target_point command, or else the centre of each part’s area. Circles round it lie row_spacing_mm apart, or graded towards end_row_spacing_mm with their distance from the centre, out to 2 spacings past the part’s farthest point, with one of 0.1 CSS pixel radius at the centre. Alternate circles are joined into a spiral in, and the rest into one back out, as the contour fill’s double spiral is, and the part cuts the spiral into pieces. One piece is sewn as a running stitch, joined along the outline to where the fill starts and ends. Several are routed as tatami rows are, with the travel under them at angle and skip_last. A part that lies within a row spacing of its centre is sewn as one circle of that radius. Bean stitch leaves the travel out, and the repeats double the stitching, as the contour fill’s do.

Guided fill (P3)

Rows that follow a guide line instead of a straight angle: the guide is copied (Copy) or offset (Parallel Offset, Buffer) across the region at row_spacing_mm, each copy clipped to the region and sewn with the tatami needle grid (staggers, stitch_position_method). Specified fully at M10.

Linear gradient fill (P3)

A tatami variant whose rows alternate between the colours of a linear gradient with a probability that follows the gradient position, producing a blend from interleaved threads; one colour block per stop. Specified at M10.

Tartan fill (P3)

A woven plaid from a stripe specification (sett): warp and weft stripes become fills at tartan_angle with rows_per_thread and optional herringbone. Specified at M10.

Cross stitch (P3)

The region is covered by grid cells (pattern_size_mm, cross_offset_mm, cross_rotation, canvas_grid_origin); a cell becomes a cross when the region covers at least fill_coverage percent of it; crosses use cross_stitch_method (simple, half, upright, double, Smyrna, flipped variants) and are routed row by row. Specified at M10.

Legacy fill (not planned)

Ink/Stitch’s first fill method, which it keeps for its older files: it gives legacy_fill to a fill whose auto_fill is off when it opens one. Its rows lie on a tatami fill’s grating, with a tatami fill’s needle points along them (staggers, skip_last), and it sews no underlay. It sews each stretch of rows that join on its own, back and forth, with no travel stitches between stretches; flip starts its rows from the other side, and reverse sews the whole fill backwards.

StitchCraft does not build it, since the method exists for Ink/Stitch’s older files. A fill set to it is sewn as a tatami fill with its settings and no underlay, and SC-W0314 says so (REQ-FILL-005). It is a tatami fill in every other way too. The element before it ends near its area, and its rows are routed and joined by travel stitches as a tatami fill’s are (DEV-FILL-014). flip and reverse are not planned, and a design that sets them gets SC-W0105. The VectorCraft plug-in’s fill dialog does not offer the method.
