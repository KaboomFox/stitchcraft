StitchCraft

Tartan fill

A plaid woven of warp and weft, sewn as rows of running stitches across the fill (P3, M10.6). A woven tartan’s threads cross over and under each other, and each colour shows in the stripes it is woven into, both ways. Rows that slant across the stripes of both directions, taken by the warp and the weft in turns, give that look. Where stripes of 2 colours cross, the warp’s rows alternate with the weft’s, mixing the colours as woven threads do. Each colour’s rows sew in that colour’s thread.

StitchCraft sews Ink/Stitch’s tartan fill as Ink/Stitch sews it, read at d59c9ab: lib/tartan/utils.py and lib/tartan/palette.py for the sett, lib/stitches/tartan_fill.py for the stitches, and the fill element’s tartan settings in lib/elements/fill_stitch.py. This page describes them in our own words (ADR-0012), and the code is written from it. Ink/Stitch was also run as a black box on setts and shapes made for the purpose. M10.6 sews straight rows. M10.7 adds the herringbone, and an editor that writes setts from Ink/Stitch’s palette code or from a threadcount of the Scottish Register of Tartans.

Colorful, Emilio 20 tartan, Kum Tsoan relief, Kum Tsoan tartan and Perspective tricolore, 5 fonts of Ink/Stitch’s library at c7e3a05, sew tartan fills. All but the last set herringbones 1.6 to 5 mm wide on some of their shapes.

A tartan reads the tatami fill’s row_spacing_mm, max_stitch_length_mm, staggers, skip_last and expand_mm, its whole underlay, the fill’s running stitch and its tolerance for the needle’s travel, and bean_stitch_repeats for its runs. Ink/Stitch’s window doesn’t show skip_last for a tartan, but its tartan reads it, and the compatibility contract says so (read_by).

The sett

The tartan setting is an object of JSON. Its palette is the code of the stripes. symmetry mirrors the sett at its ends, or else it repeats. scale scales every width, in percent, rotate turns the whole sett, in degrees, offset_x and offset_y move it, in millimetres, and a stripe no wider than min_stripe_width sews as a run. A key that is missing takes the value Ink/Stitch’s editor starts from: no turn or offset, mirrored, at 100 %, and runs for stripes of 1 mm or less. The editor’s other keys, such as equal_warp_weft and output, don’t change what is sewn and are passed over. Ink/Stitch stops with an error on a sett that isn’t JSON or lacks a key it reads. StitchCraft takes the editor’s value for a missing key, and skips the element for the rest, with SC-E0323, which says why.

The palette code. Each stripe is a colour in brackets, then its width in millimetres: (#0a55a3)/2.4 (#f0f0f0)0.4 (#ed171f)/2.4. The colour is # with 3 or 6 hexadecimal digits, or a colour name of SVG. A ? before the width leaves the stripe unsewn, and a * sews it in the colour of the last stripe before it sewn as a fill. A | parts the warp’s stripes from the weft’s, and without one the weft is the warp. Ink/Stitch takes the stripes wherever they lie in the text and passes over what lies between them, such as slashes and the dots of a repeated sett. Its editor writes a repeated sett’s code as a list, ...['(#00673e)5.0', '(#000000)1.5']..., which reads as its stripes. A colour that can’t be read leaves its stripe unsewn, as Ink/Stitch leaves it, with SC-W0328. A code with more than one |, or a width that isn’t a number, can’t be read.

Colour letters. A code without brackets is a threadcount. Each of its stripes is a run of colour letters followed by its count of threads, as in B/24 W4 R2, and a ? or a * may come between them. Letters without a count are passed over, and the fill reads each thread as 0.5 mm wide, as Ink/Stitch’s tartan does. The letters, in capitals or not, stand for the shades the Scottish Register of Tartans publishes in “Tartan Register colours” (https://www.tartanregister.gov.uk/docs/Colour_shades.pdf), B for blue and DG for dark green. The list gives several shades for most of its 25 letters. StitchCraft’s table (generators::tartan::shades) holds, for each letter, the shade of the list that Ink/Stitch sews, so that a sett written in letters sews the same threads in both programs. The list is Crown copyright. The register’s copyright page (https://www.tartanregister.gov.uk/copyright) lets its text be reused with an acknowledgement, which NOTICE gives. Letters the register uses for no colour leave their stripe unsewn, with SC-W0328.

The register’s threadcount. The register’s search gives a sett as a threadcount with a palette of its own. A line saying Threadcount: comes before the stripes, letters and counts run together as K6R20K2Y4, and a line saying Pallet: before each letter’s colour, as K=101010BLACK;. A code that says Threadcount reads so. Its counts come from the line after the last Threadcount: line. Each letter’s colour comes from the line after the last Pallet: line, whose letters, =, a # or none and 6 hexadecimal digits name a colour. There, letters without a count make the code unreadable. With a | anywhere in a code of letters, every stripe is the warp’s and the weft has none, as in Ink/Stitch.

An empty setting sews Ink/Stitch’s default sett, (#101010)/5.0 (#FFFFFF)/?5.0: stripes 5 mm wide of a thread almost black, with 5 mm left unsewn between them. SC-W0325 says so. VectorCraft’s fill dialog shows the setting as text, and StitchCraft Tartan, a filter, writes it from a code (the editor).

A direction’s width. Ink/Stitch measures each direction’s stripes, once scaled: the stripes as wide as min_stripe_width or wider add up, but for those sewn in the colour before them. A direction without such a stripe measures its narrower stripes that are sewn instead. The wider direction’s width widens the box the stripes are laid over (below). A direction that measures 0, or has no stripe sewn in its own colour, sews nothing, and a sett whose 2 directions sew nothing sews its underlay alone, with SC-W0326.

The full sett

The stripes, scaled, become a sequence of bands, each of a colour or of none, and runs, each a line in a colour between 2 bands.

  • Runs. A stripe sewn in its own colour no wider than min_stripe_width sews as a run along its middle. Ink/Stitch compares the 2 widths in CSS pixels, and StitchCraft compares them the same way, so a stripe at the limit falls the same side. Half its width goes to the band before it, and half to those after it. Ink/Stitch adds that half to each band that follows, not to the next alone. It stops at a band left unsewn or sewn in the colour before it, which takes the half, or where the next run gives its own half instead. A run at the end leaves its half as a band of its own. StitchCraft lays the bands so too. Ink/Stitch, run as a black box on setts with runs among their stripes and at their ends, mirrored or repeated, lays the half more than once.
  • The colour round a run. The band before a run is in the colour of the last band sewn as a fill. A run before any such band lies in the colour of the first stripe wider than the narrowest for a mirrored sett, which it goes back to, and of the last for a repeated one, whose pass before ends with it.
  • Mirrored. With symmetry on, the sequence goes back from its last step but one to its second. The 2 stripes it turns at are then sewn once, as is a run it turns at. Then the whole repeats.

Stripes and rows

Each part of the fill is grown by expand_mm and cut into the pieces that leaves, as a tatami fill’s parts are. Each piece gets its stripes and rows of its own.

The box. The stripes cover a square round the middle of the piece’s bounds. It reaches as far from the middle as the radius of the smallest circle round the piece, plus a margin. The margin is the wider direction’s width or the width of 2 rows per thread of rows, whichever is more. Its left and top sides move out by the sizes of offset_x and offset_y. The circle’s radius is found from the piece’s convex hull, by the randomized incremental method (Welzl’s), its points shuffled by a fixed seed so that it is the same every time.

The stripes. The warp’s full sett is laid from the box’s left side as upright bands, and the weft’s from its top as level bands, one after another until a band starts past the far side. Bands of one colour that meet are one band. The whole is turned by rotate degrees about the middle of the bounds, counter-clockwise in y-up axes, as Ink/Stitch’s geometry library turns it. The canvas shows y down, and there a positive turn shows clockwise. StitchCraft works in the sett’s own frame. It turns the piece back by the sett’s angle, which makes each band a slab between 2 lines square to an axis, and then turns the lines it makes forward again.

The rows run across the box at tartan_angle to the stripes, from its turned left side to its turned right side. The left side’s length is cut into equal steps no longer than the row spacing, and a row starts at each step. Along a row, needle points lie the longest stitch apart, from a staggers-th of a stitch further on than the row before’s, and both its ends are needle points too. A row with room for fewer than 2 needle points between its ends has its ends alone. The rows go to the warp and the weft in turns of rows_per_thread. Ink/Stitch sorts each direction’s rows by their starts, x first, which takes them from one end of the left side or the other, as the sett’s angle and the rows’ angle turn it. Every other row of a direction runs back, and its rows zigzag.

A colour’s lines

In each band, each row of the band’s direction that crosses it for more than half a CSS pixel gives its stretch inside the band, with the needle points that lie there. Each stretch is joined to the next by a straight line along the band’s edge, in equal stitches no longer than the running stitch, unless either end lies within 0.005 pixel of the piece’s rings. So a band’s rows make one zigzag, which breaks at the outline. Only the stretches that touch the piece’s bounds count, with one more either side, whose joins may cross into it. The zigzag is cut where it leaves the piece. Where a line ends inside the piece, more than 0.05 pixel from its rings, it runs on from that end along the band’s edge to the nearest ring, in the running stitch’s length.

The runs lie along their stripes’ middles across the box, cut by the piece. A piece of 0.5 mm or less is left out. Each piece is cut into equal stitches no longer than the running stitch, along the warp, and the longest stitch, along the weft, as Ink/Stitch cuts them.

Each colour’s lines are its bands’ in the warp, then in the weft. The colours come in the order of their first band or run in the warp’s sett, and then come the weft’s colours that the warp has none of.

Herringbone

With herringbone_width_mm above 0, the rows lie in bands that wide (M10.7). A herringbone twill’s ribs turn at the edge of each band, and the cloth shows columns of chevrons: each band’s rows slant the other way from the band before’s, and the weft’s rows run on from band to band. Ink/Stitch lays them so in lib/stitches/tartan_fill.py, read at d59c9ab. Colorful, Emilio 20 tartan, Kum Tsoan relief and Kum Tsoan tartan set herringbones 1.6 to 5 mm wide on 61 of their tartan fills.

The bands. In the sett’s frame, before it is turned, the bands stand upright side by side from the box’s left side, 0.005 CSS pixel apart so that no 2 bands’ rows meet. Each band is the herringbone’s width, and bands are laid while one starts before the box’s right side. The bands turn with the sett. Their rows cross the stripes at 45°, whatever tartan_angle is.

A band’s rows join its 2 sides, each as long as the box is high plus the band’s width and cut into equal steps no longer than the row spacing. A row goes from each step of the left side to the same step of the right side. In the first band, the third and so on, the left side starts a band’s width above the box, and the rows run down to the right, as the canvas shows them. In the others it starts at the box’s top, and the rows run up to the right. The bands so share their rows, each band’s moved across and slanted its own way, and StitchCraft works them out once. Along each row the needle points are staggered from the band’s first row, as straight rows’ are. In the first band, the third and so on, the first rows_per_thread rows of every 2 × rows_per_thread go to the weft and the others to the warp, and the other way round in the other bands. Each direction’s rows of a band are taken in the order straight rows are, every other one sewn back.

The warp is laid band by band. The second band, the fourth and so on come first, and then the first, the third and so on. In each band, each warp stripe’s rows are joined along the stripe’s edges and cut by the piece, as straight rows are, but a line that ends inside the piece doesn’t run on to its rings. A warp colour comes in the order of its first line.

The weft is joined across the bands. Each band’s weft rows are first joined end to start into one zigzag, in stitches no longer than the running stitch. Each weft stripe that lies over the piece cuts every band’s zigzag, and a band’s pieces in the stripe longer than half a CSS pixel are joined along the stripe’s edges, unless an end lies on the piece’s rings. While an edge of the stripe crosses the piece, the bands’ lines in the stripe are then joined into one, from the band at one end of the stripe to the band at the other. Ink/Stitch takes the stripe’s edges from its outline as its geometry library orders that outline, from the corner of least x. With the sett turned less than a quarter turn either way, or a quarter turn exactly, the stripe’s lower edge, as the sett’s frame shows it with y down, runs to the right, and the bands are taken from the right. Otherwise its upper edge runs to the left, and they are taken from the left. The end of the first band’s last line farther along that edge goes on to the next band. Each line after it starts at its end on the edge nearer where the line before ended, the first edge on a tie, and is joined to it along that edge. Ink/Stitch moves the weft’s lines right and down by 0.00123 pixel before it cuts them by the piece, so that a line along the outline falls inside or outside it as there, and StitchCraft moves them as much. Every weft colour with a stripe over the piece comes after the warp’s colours, lines or none.

Where a line meets itself. Ink/Stitch’s geometry library breaks a weft line where it finds the line meets itself as it cuts the line by the piece. Near the outline a weft line can run twice along the same stretch of a stripe’s edge, and whether the library finds a meeting there hangs on the last bits of its arithmetic. StitchCraft sews such a line whole (DEV-FILL-030).

What it costs. The budget is charged for every band’s rows as if each were made, their needle points, each stripe each band is tested against, and each point of a zigzag, before memory is taken for them.

The editor

Ink/Stitch’s tartan editor makes a sett from stripes picked one by one, or reads a code pasted into it, and saves the tartan setting (lib/gui/tartan/ and lib/tartan/palette.py, read at d59c9ab). StitchCraft’s editor (generators::tartan::editor, M10.7) does what it does with a code.

  • Reading. A code reads as the fill reads it (above), but each thread of a threadcount is as wide as the editor is told. Ink/Stitch’s editor starts at 0.2 mm, where its fill reads a thread as 0.5 mm. Whether the sett is mirrored comes from the code. A palette code or colour letters between the dots of a repeated sett, ..., repeat, and so does a register’s threadcount that says full sett. The others are mirrored. The colours that can’t be read are named. The sett starts unturned, unscaled and unmoved, with stripes of 1 mm or less sewn as runs.
  • The code it writes. Each stripe is its colour in brackets, # and 6 hexadecimal digits, a ? before the width of a stripe left unsewn and a * before one sewn in the colour before it, and its width in millimetres, to a millionth. The first stripe and the last are marked / when the sett is mirrored, and the whole lies between ... when it repeats. A | comes before the weft’s stripes when they aren’t the warp’s.
  • The setting it writes is the object of JSON Ink/Stitch’s editor saves: symmetry, equal_warp_weft, rotate, scale, offset_x, offset_y, palette and min_stripe_width, in that order. The fill reads it back as the sett.

In VectorCraft the fill dialog holds the tartan setting as text, shown for the tartan fill alone, and StitchCraft Tartan, a filter, writes it from a code, the width of a thread and how the sett is laid. A live effect is given only the settings its manifest declares, so without the text no preview could sew a sett, and a code is easier to get right than an object of JSON. The text needs VectorCraft 0.14, and the filter 0.15. Why both, and the choices left aside, are in VectorCraft integration.

Sewing

A tartan sews its parts in a tatami fill’s order, nearest the needle first (fills). Each part sews the tatami fill’s underlay first, in the element’s own thread. Then each of its pieces sews its colours.

The colours sewn as fills come in the order above. Their last colour that has runs too moves to the end of them, so the thread doesn’t change between its rows and its runs. Each colour’s lines are routed as a tatami fill’s rows are routed (routing) and sewn once. Between them the needle runs along the piece’s outline, never under the rows. A colour goes from where the colour before it ended, or the needle, towards the end that the next part or element offers. The travel before its first line and after its last is left out, so it starts and ends with its rows. Where the needle or the end is missing, the colour starts or ends at the outline’s second point, as in Ink/Stitch. Its geometry library rebuilds the outline, which then starts one point on from where the drawing starts it, so that point is the outline’s third here.

With stop_at_ending_point on, every colour keeps its travel. Each starts where the needle was when the piece’s colours began, after its underlay, and travels on to the end.

The runs come after every colour sewn as a fill, the colour they share with the fills first. Each colour’s runs are routed the same way, from where the colour before ended towards the end. Their travel is kept, and bean_stitch_repeats sews each of their stitches back and forth but the travel’s.

Several parts. Each part sews its underlay and then its colours. Then, as Ink/Stitch sorts a tartan’s groups, its underlays and colours sewn as fills are ordered by where their colour first comes among them, then its runs the same way, so that each thread sews in one stretch. An underlay in a colour a fill sews too sorts with that colour.

Threads and colour blocks. Each colour’s lines, and each colour’s runs, are a colour block of their own thread, which assembly trims after when the element’s trim_after is on. The underlay sews in the element’s thread and ends no colour block (several threads in one element). A colour of the element’s own sews in the element’s thread, named or not, and the others in threads of their colours alone. A named and an unnamed thread of one colour would be 2 threads. A PES file writes them as 2 entries of its palette, and the second is another colour. The sewing order reads a tartan’s threads from its settings alone, before it is sewn: its own thread for the underlay, then the colours above.

Stitches. A needle point nearer the one before than the element’s shortest stitch is left out, as finalize would leave it out. Where the rows cross a stripe sewn as a fill in a stretch shorter than the shortest stitch, a warp stripe’s width over the cosine of the rows’ angle or a weft stripe’s over its sine, SC-W0327 says needle points of it are left out. A herringbone’s rows cross the stripes at 45°, and a row crosses no more of a stripe than its band’s width: a herringbone narrower than the narrowest stripe is said instead. The budget is charged for the sett’s steps, the rows and their needle points, the bands each row is tested against and the stitches, before memory is taken for them. A sett too fine for the budget ends the element with SC-E0004.

A preview too costly to sew shows each colour’s lines and runs, without the stitches.

How it compares with Ink/Stitch

The differential sews TS-17B and 4 files of its own in both programs, and they agree by every measure (REQ-FILL-TRT-011). Their colour blocks are the same, in the same threads, with the same trims and jumps, and each starts and ends at the same points. StitchCraft sews 2 to 6 % fewer stitches. It leaves out needle points nearer each other than the machine’s shortest stitch, 0.3 mm, where Ink/Stitch leaves out those within 0.1 mm (DEV-FIN-001): most of them are the stitches where a row enters or leaves a band. At tartan_angle 0, the joins between rows along a band’s edge are a row spacing long, 0.25 mm, and a probe square sewed 13.5 % fewer stitches for it. Between their runs, the 2 programs’ needles can travel along different stretches of the outline, where they pair the graph’s odd nodes differently (DEV-FILL-006). Ink/Stitch’s geometry library divides a run’s length by the stitch length in pixels, and where the quotient is a whole number its rounding can give one stitch more. A run 40 mm long in stitches of 2.5 mm takes 17 stitches there, against 16 here.

The differential’s herringbones agree too: TS-17B’s, and a file of 4 of them, turned both ways, round a hole, with a warp and a weft apart and with stripes sewn as runs. Their weft joins its rows along the bands’ sides in steps of the row spacing, so StitchCraft leaves out more of Ink/Stitch’s short stitches there. The file of 4 excuses its count of stitches by DEV-FIN-001. TS-17B’s whole count agrees within the differential’s 10 % all the same, its gradients’ stitches counted with its tartans’ (M10.12).

The glyph differential sews the tartan fonts’ 313 glyphs too. Their files are older than Ink/Stitch’s version 4, and there a fill without a longest stitch of its own sews stitches 3 mm long, as Ink/Stitch updates it (files of older versions). All of them agree, or differ only as the ledger explains, the 35 that set a herringbone among them. Colorful’s F breaks a weft line where Ink/Stitch’s geometry library finds it meets itself (DEV-FILL-030), and Colorful’s ’ starts its satin column’s rings where its rungs pair them (DEV-SAT-009).

Test sheet

TS-17B sews 6 tartans 40 mm square, each trimmed after its colours (machine testing). One sett is mirrored at its ends, and one is repeated, written as Ink/Stitch’s editor writes it. One has a warp and a weft apart, and one has stripes 0.5 and 0.8 mm wide sewn as runs. One is turned 30° and scaled by 150 %, and the last is a herringbone 3 mm wide. Each underlay sews in a colour of its sett other than its first stripe’s, so the underlay is a colour block of its own: in the first stripe’s colour, the jump from the underlay to the rows would leave its thread under them on the reference machine. Every stripe is one of Brother’s threads, so both programs’ files name each thread as itself. VectorCraft’s Test Sheet filter draws TS-17B too, as its fill dialog shows a sett as text from M10.7.
