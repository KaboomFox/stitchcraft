StitchCraft

Guided fill

Rows that bend with a line the designer draws beside the fill (P3, M10.9). The guide line says how the rows run, so that they can follow a shape that curves, and the fill copies it across each part a row spacing at a time. StitchCraft sews Ink/Stitch’s copy strategy as Ink/Stitch sews it (read at d59c9ab, lib/stitches/guided_fill.py and the fill element’s guided fill settings in lib/elements/fill_stitch.py, described here in our own words, ADR-0012). Ink/Stitch’s parallel offset and buffer strategies come with M10.10. Until then a fill set to one of them is skipped, and SC-W0011 names the strategy (REQ-FILL-GDD-001).

The guide line

The guide line is a path grouped with the fill and marked as a guide, and is never stitched itself. In an SVG file Ink/Stitch’s guide-line marker starts it. Of the paths so marked in its group, the fill takes the first that isn’t a satin column, since Ink/Stitch’s fill looks past satin guides, which steer ripple stitches (REQ-SVG-015). VectorCraft documents name it stitch:guide, the marker a ripple stitch reads too (REQ-VC-023). Only the marker’s first subpath is copied.

The engine makes that subpath one open line before it copies it (REQ-FILL-GDD-002):

  1. Flattened as a fill’s outline is.
  2. Made one line. A copy of a line that crosses itself crosses itself too, and its rows would cross each other. So the line is cut into pieces where it crosses or touches itself. A piece that comes back to where it began, a loop, is left out, and 2 pieces that meet end to end where no third meets them are joined. This is done up to 4 times, until one line is left. A line still in pieces after that, or one that runs back along itself, is replaced by the straight line from its first point to its last.
  3. Opened. A closed line has no line between its ends to copy it by, so its last 2 points are left out: the end it closes on and the point before it.
  4. Lengthened straight on at both ends, along the line from its first point to its last. Each end goes out by the larger of the distances from the 2 ends to the corner of the part’s bounds farthest from each. Every copy then starts and ends outside the part, however far it is moved.

After step 2, a guide of no length, or one drawn there and back, starts and ends at one point. It doesn’t give a line to copy it by, and the fill is sewn as a tatami fill with its own settings, as Ink/Stitch sews a guided fill without a guide. SC-W0331 says why (DEV-FILL-034). Ink/Stitch moves each lengthened end by up to 0.01 px at random, and StitchCraft doesn’t (DEV-FILL-035).

The copies

The copies move a row spacing at a time. Without guided_fill_angle they move square to the line between the guide’s ends, turned a right angle clockwise from it as the drawing shows it. With it, they move at that angle, clockwise from the x axis as drawn. Copy k lies k row spacings from the guide that way, so an angle near the guide’s own direction moves the copies mostly along it, and brings the rows closer together than the row spacing (REQ-FILL-GDD-003).

Where the lengthened guide meets the part, the copies start from it, copy 0. Elsewhere they start from the copy nearest the part’s centroid. Its number counts the whole row spacings, rounded down, from the nearest point of the lengthened guide to the centroid. The number is negative where the centroid lies behind the guide as the copies move. From there the copies go on outward, one way and then the other, until one misses the box round the part. The copies that meet the part are its rows, those that touch its outline among them. With smoothness_mm each copy is smoothed first, as a contour fill’s path is smoothed (REQ-FILL-GDD-004).

The rows are then taken in the order of their first points. Of 2 rows, the one with the smaller x comes first, and then the one with the smaller y. More rows are taken by how far along the diagonal of the box round their first points each lies, from the box’s corner of least x and y. Rows as far along keep the order they were found in. That order counts each row’s stagger and keys its random lengths.

The stitches

Row i, from 0 in the order the rows are taken, is smoothed again by smoothness_mm and stitched as stitch_position_method says (REQ-FILL-GDD-005). It keeps its first point, and its needle points start i / staggers of a longest stitch along it, with its whole turns left out. With 4 staggers, the second row starts a quarter of a stitch on, the third half a stitch on, and the fifth where the first did. Staggers of 0 count as 1, where Ink/Stitch divides by them and stops (DEV-FILL-033). Each row is lengthened well past the part. Its needle points keep their place inside it.

A row’s corners are where Ink/Stitch’s running stitch takes them. A join that turns by 45° or more is one, unless it lies within twice running_stitch_tolerance_mm along the row of the turn before it. The gentler joins where a copy meets its lengthened ends aren’t corners. The stagger runs on through them.

  • Flexible, the default, spreads a row’s stitches evenly between its corners, at most the longest stitch each. The rows’ needle points keep their stagger near the start of each row, and drift from it where a row’s stitches are spread.
  • Flexible (stagger) sews whole longest stitches from where the stagger starts the row, a needle point on each corner, and the last stitch whatever is left. The stagger then lasts the whole row long.

With random lengths on, the stitches are drawn as a running stitch draws them, from a stream of the element’s seed that the row’s number keys, whatever the position method. Either way, a stitch that strays from its row by more than the tolerance is split there, as a running stitch’s is.

Cutting the rows

The stitched row is cut by the part (REQ-FILL-GDD-006). It is cut at every point where it crosses one of the part’s rings or touches one, but not where it runs along one. For such a stretch Ink/Stitch’s geometry library gives a line, and Ink/Stitch cuts at points only. A stretch between 2 cuts, or between a cut and the row’s end, is a line of the fill when the part contains its middle fifth, from 40 % to 60 % of its length. That fifth lies nowhere outside the part, and partly inside it, off the rings. Each line runs the row’s way and keeps the row’s needle points between its cuts.

Sewing the lines

A guided fill sews its parts as a tatami fill sews them, nearest the needle first, each with the tatami fill’s underlay before its lines and grown by expand_mm for them (REQ-FILL-GDD-007). The shared part loop does that (section 5). Its lines are routed as a circular fill’s pieces are, each a row of the tatami fill’s route between its ends (a circular fill’s sewing). Each is sewn once with all its needle points, from the end the route enters it by, and without its last with skip_last. From line to line the needle goes as a tatami fill’s does, under the lines not sewn yet with underpath on. The lines it follows under them are turned from guided_fill_angle, or from 0 when it is empty, as Ink/Stitch turns them.

With bean_stitch_repeats, the stitches shorter than the shortest stitch are left out first, so that each stitch the bean stitch sews back over is one the machine sews. Then each stitch of the lines is sewn back and forth, and the travel between them is not.

Fallbacks

The copies can miss a part thinner than the row spacing between 2 of them. Such a part is sewn as a tatami fill at the angle of the line between the guide’s ends. Ink/Stitch falls back to one where its copies meet a part but the cut keeps none of them, and where no copy meets the part at all, it stops with an error first (DEV-FILL-034). The tatami fill’s rows lie the guided fill’s row spacing apart, in its longest stitch with its staggers and skip_last. It has no end spacing, random lengths, pull compensation or rows that fill gaps, which Ink/Stitch’s fallback doesn’t pass on. SC-W0332 counts those parts, and SC-W0305 those too thin for the tatami rows too, which are sewn round their outline.

Preview

A preview too costly to sew shows the segments the lines inside each part run along. A part that falls back to a tatami fill shows its rows. The preview is given the element’s random generator, so that random lengths cut the lines where sewing would.

Cost

Each point of the guide, of its copies and of their stitches charges a unit of work. Looking for where the guide meets itself tests only the sides whose spans along x overlap, a unit for each pair. Testing a copy against the part, and cutting a row by it, look only at the sides of the rings near each stitch. Sewing charges a stitch for each needle point, and a preview’s budget allows any number of them.
