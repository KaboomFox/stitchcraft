StitchCraft

Stroke generators

Strokes follow a path. Shape: StrokePath (an ordered list of polylines, corners marked, from the normalizer). Phase P1 (M3) unless noted.

A stroke starts where it is drawn and ends where it is drawn, as in Ink/Stitch. Its generator is given its neighbours, as every generator is (ADR 0014), and a stroke sews the same whatever they are, and whatever start and end positions its design gives it (REQ-GEN-001). A satin column or a tatami fill before a stroke ends near the stroke’s first point.

A stroke cut to clips, its SVG clip paths or VectorCraft clipping objects, sews the pieces of its lines inside every one (REQ-GEN-005). Its subpaths are cut where they cross a clip’s outline, as Ink/Stitch cuts them. A running stitch’s or a zigzag stroke’s is flattened first as a shape’s outline is, and a manual stitch’s runs from node to node, with a needle point at each cut. Each piece sews on its own, in order along its subpath, and a closed subpath that is cut is cut where it starts too. Left whole, a subpath keeps its curves and sews as drawn. The clips leave the width alone, so a zigzag stroke’s zigzag ends square at a cut, half its width either side of the line. Ink/Stitch closes each piece of a cut closed zigzag stroke, which StitchCraft sews open (DEV-RUN-009), and sews a line that also touches its clip at a point whole, which StitchCraft cuts as any other (DEV-RUN-010). Wholly outside its clips, a stroke isn’t sewn (SC-W0013), and the element before it ends nearest the first piece they leave. A ripple stitch cuts its needle points instead, and routes the pieces (clips).

Running stitch

The workhorse: outlines, details, travel, underlay.

Parameters: running_stitch_length_mm (one length or a repeating pattern of lengths, LengthList), running_stitch_tolerance_mm, repeats, bean_stitch_repeats, enable_random_stitch_length, random_stitch_length_jitter_percent, random_seed.

Placement

A stitch is the straight line between two needle points, so placement measures along the path but checks straight. “The shortest stitch” is the one Finalize enforces (settings or profile, whichever is larger).

  1. Flatten. The normalizer turns the path into polylines within a tenth of the tolerance (the other nine tenths are left for the stitches) and marks its corners: joins between segments where the path turns by more than 30°. A curve’s own bend is never a corner, however tight.
  2. Split at corners. Every corner gets a needle penetration, so sharp shapes stay sharp, unless it is closer than the shortest stitch, along the path, to the previous penetration or to the end: then the spans on either side of it are joined.
  3. Even spacing per span. For a span of length L between corners and target length s, place n = ceil(L / s − ε) stitches spaced L / n apart along the path. Every stitch is at most s and the last one is never a short leftover — the classic even-division rule digitizers use. With several lengths ("2.5 1.0") the lengths repeat along the path, carrying on from span to span; each span takes as many as it needs and scales them by one factor to end exactly on its corner. Lengths below twice the shortest stitch are raised to it (SC-W0402), so a span longer than the longest length scales its stitches by more than a half and keeps each at or above the shortest stitch; in a shorter span, a stitch scaled below it joins its shorter neighbour. Where those stitches stray from the path by more than the tolerance left after flattening, as on a curve too tight for them, the span is spread again in one stitch more, then one more, as long as that takes no more stitches than step 5 would make of it, until they keep to it. A small circle is so sewn in even stitches as long as the tolerance left after flattening allows, where splitting the stray ones would halve them. With random length, each try draws its lengths again from where the span’s draws began.
  4. Measure straight. Where the path bends back on itself within less than the shortest stitch (a cusp, a tight loop, a curl at an end), two points far apart along it can be close in a straight line. Going along the needle points: one closer than the shortest stitch to the last one kept is dropped; a corner drops the points kept since the previous corner instead, and is dropped itself if that is not enough; the end drops whatever it takes, corners too. A drop lengthens the stitch over it, and one longer than the longest length is split where the path is half its length from its start (at most the longest length), as often as needed. When only the start is left and the end is too close to it (a small closed loop), the stitch goes by way of the point of the path farthest from both ends; if even that is closer than the shortest stitch, the part is not stitched (SC-W0401).
  5. Respect the curve. If the chord between two consecutive needle points strays from the path by more than the tolerance left after flattening, split that stitch at the path’s point of greatest deviation, among those at least the shortest stitch from both its ends, and repeat. A part longer than the longest length is split along the path as in step 4. So the stitches stray from the path by at most running_stitch_tolerance_mm, except where the path’s details are smaller than the shortest stitch. There a point of the flattened path that is passed over lies nearer than the shortest stitch to a needle of its stitch, and the path strays by less than the shortest stitch plus a tenth of the tolerance.
  6. Random length. With enable_random_stitch_length, each stitch length is drawn uniformly from s × (1 ± jitter), the first of each span a random fraction of that (a random phase, below), then the span is rescaled to end on its corner. Each span draws from its own stream of the element’s generator, keyed by its subpath’s place in the path and its own place in the subpath, so a change to one span leaves the lengths of the others as they were. The generator is seeded with the element’s random_seed (a setting every stitch type that varies at random shares), or with its id when that is empty. A seed replaces the id, as in Ink/Stitch, so elements alike given one seed sew alike, and the same element and seed always give the same stitches.

When the rules disagree, the shortest stitch wins (a shorter stitch hammers one spot and can break the thread), then corners, then the tolerance. A part of the path that is a single point, shorter than the shortest stitch, or lying all within it of its ends, is not stitched (SC-W0401). Only arithmetic and square roots are used, so every platform places the same stitches.

Compared with Ink/Stitch: the same parameters, meanings and defaults; the placement differs in four documented ways, DEV-RUN-001 to DEV-RUN-004 in the deviations ledger (conformance/deviations.toml), and random length draws and places its lengths in other ways (DEV-RUN-005).

Satin underlays walk lines made point by point by the satin generator (satin), and steps 2 to 5 place their stitches, with each walk’s own length and tolerance. Such a line is a polyline already, and each join that turns by more than 30° is a corner. A line too small for a stitch of the shortest length is sewn as its 2 ends, as Ink/Stitch sews it, and finalizing merges what is too short.

A wide stroke’s area, which automatic digitizing fills (image to embroidery), is made from the path segment by segment, flattened within the shapes’ tolerance: its joins go where 2 segments meet, and it bends smoothly inside a curve. Its ends and corners are square to the chords there, and half the stroke’s width turns the angle such a chord makes with its curve into a shift of the corner. So a curve that ends the subpath, or meets the next segment at a corner, is halved further at that end until the shift is within the tolerance too. Where 2 segments meet smoothly nothing more is needed.

Repeats and bean stitch

  • repeats = k sews the path k times, alternating direction; odd k ends at the far end, even k returns to the start (useful for travel that must come back). Each pass starts where the last one ended, so a turnaround is not a stitch in place.
  • bean_stitch_repeats = b replaces every stitch A→B with A→B→A→B… (2b+1 passes), producing the thick “triple stitch” line at b = 1. A list ("1 0") alternates bean and plain stitches.
  • Order, as in Ink/Stitch (read at d59c9ab): bean stitch applies after repeats, to every stitch of every pass, with the list running on from pass to pass. Ink/Stitch repeats the turnaround point, so the turnaround takes one step of the list; StitchCraft counts that step too, without sewing it, so a file sews the same in both. With a two-value list, each stretch of the path then gets the same treatment on every pass.
  • A zigzag’s turnaround takes no step of the list, as in Ink/Stitch (zigzag stroke).
  • Repeats apply to running, ripple and zigzag strokes; bean stitch to those and to manual stitch (the compatibility contract lists each parameter’s stitch types).
  • The fills sewn as one path have repeats and bean stitch of their own, which Ink/Stitch declares for its fills apart from a stroke’s. Bean stitch comes first, and each repeat then sews everything sewn so far again, the other way round, so k repeats sew 2^(k−1) passes (fills).
  • A circular fill’s bean stitch leaves out the stitches that run between the pieces of its spiral, but each of those stitches takes a step of the list, as in Ink/Stitch (circular fill). A tartan fill takes the fills’ bean stitch for its runs alone, the needle’s travel between them left out the same way, and its rows and its needle’s travel take the running stitch’s length, as a tatami fill’s travel does (tartan fill).

Random length

Ink/Stitch draws each stitch from s × (1 ± jitter) and starts each stretch between corners at a random phase, so rows sewn side by side with the same settings do not line their holes up. One random number gives a stitch both its length and its phase: the first stitch of a stretch is its drawn length times that number. After a stitch that the tolerance made shorter, the next starts a new phase too. Ink/Stitch does not rescale, so the last stitch before a corner is whatever is left. Each stretch draws from a stream keyed by the seed and the stretch’s place in its subpath, and the places start again in each subpath, so subpaths drawn alike sew alike.

StitchCraft draws a stitch’s length and its span’s phase from 2 numbers. A phase starts only where a span starts, and each span is rescaled to end on its corner (placement, step 6: no short leftover). Its streams are keyed by the subpath’s place as well, so subpaths drawn alike, such as lines side by side, start at different phases, which is what the random phase is for (DEV-RUN-005).

A contour fill sews its path stretch by stretch, a ring or a turn of a spiral at a time, each a span of its own, and keys each span’s streams by its number in the fill (fills).

Properties (conformance)

  • Stitches are spread evenly between corners, in more of them where they stray from the points of the flattened path by more than the tolerance left after flattening, as long as that takes no more than splitting. Measured straight, every stitch is at most s × (1 + jitter) + 1 µm for the longest length s and at least the shortest stitch, and a part too small for that is reported (REQ-RUN-001).
  • Deviation from the source path ≤ tolerance, both ways, measured densely (REQ-RUN-002).
  • Corners are penetration points unless that would make a stitch too short (REQ-RUN-003).
  • Bean stitch sews each stitch 2b + 1 times, so a run of n stitches with b throughout has exactly n × (2b + 1); the list runs on across repeats, each turnaround taking a step (REQ-RUN-004).
  • Repeats alternate direction with no stitch in place; their parity decides the exit point (REQ-RUN-005).
  • With random length the lengths vary. The same element and seed give the same stitches, and another seed gives others. A seed replaces the element’s id, and elements given one seed draw the same lengths. Each span draws from its own stream, so a change to one span leaves the others’ lengths as they were (REQ-RUN-007).

Diagnostics

SC-W0401 part of the path too small for the shortest stitch (skipped); SC-W0402 stitch length below twice the shortest stitch (raised), a stroke’s or a satin underlay’s, which the message names.

Manual stitch

Every node of the path is a needle penetration, in order — for hand-placed stitches and imported stitch files. A curve gives only its end node: its control points are not stitched and it is not flattened. When the path is closed it comes back to its start, and a node where the needle already is sews no stitch. Segments longer than max_stitch_length_mm (if set, a setting manual stitch shares with satin columns) are split into the fewest equal parts no longer than it, but never into parts shorter than the shortest stitch, and SC-W0404 says how many stitches stay longer for that. Ink/Stitch splits a segment into the fewest equal parts no longer than the setting, however short they are (DEV-RUN-006). A value of 0 means “not set”, as in Ink/Stitch, and so does one below 0, so every stitch is then sewn as drawn. Bean stitch applies, and repeats do not. Lock stitches are added only when force_lock_stitches is set, as in Ink/Stitch (read at d59c9ab).

Ink/Stitch’s bean stitch takes a step of its list for every point it lists, and it lists some where the needle already is. A node drawn twice is listed twice, and so is the end of each stitch Ink/Stitch splits. Where a closed path came back to its start already, the start is listed once more. Its stitch plan leaves out stitches shorter than its shortest stitch only after the bean stitch, and a node left out for that keeps its step. StitchCraft gives each of these its step, sewing nothing, and a bean list then falls on the same stitches in both tools.

The shortest stitch holds here too: a node closer than it to the needle point before it is left out, so the stitch before runs on to the next node, and SC-W0403 says how many. A part’s last node is always kept, leaving out the one before it instead. Ink/Stitch drops such points later, over the whole plan, without a word, and only those 0.1 mm or less from the point before at its default (DEV-RUN-006). A part that is one point, or too small for one stitch, is not stitched (SC-W0401).

Properties: needle points are the nodes in order plus only the documented splits, and SC-W0404 names a stitch kept longer than max_stitch_length_mm (REQ-RUN-006). Every stitch is at least as long as the shortest stitch (REQ-RUN-008).

Zigzag stroke (P2, M7)

A zigzag along the path, as wide as its stroke. The needle crosses the path from side to side, as Ink/Stitch sews its zig-zag stitch (stroke_method zigzag_stitch, read at d59c9ab). It suits a light border, a couched look or a stem too thin for a satin column.

Parameters: zigzag_spacing_mm, the distance along the path from one stitch to the next that crosses the same way, a zig and a zag together, 0.4 mm by default; stroke_pull_compensation_mm, how much further out than half the stroke’s width each side’s needle points go, one value for both sides or 2; and zigzag_angle, which turns every stitch away from square to the path, from −89° to 89°. repeats and bean_stitch_repeats apply. The width is the stroke’s, read as a satin column drawn as one path reads it. Before Ink/Stitch’s file version 3 a zigzag stroke read pull_compensation_mm, shared by its 2 sides, and in a file of version 1 or 2 the SVG reader halves it into stroke_pull_compensation_mm, as Ink/Stitch’s update does (REQ-SVG-007).

Strokes, satin columns and fills each read zigzag_spacing_mm their own way (parameter registry, rule 7). A stroke reads a list, as Ink/Stitch does. Only its first value counts.

Placement

Each part of the path, one per subpath, is sewn on its own, as a running stitch is.

  1. Spread. The part is flattened within a tenth of a CSS pixel, as a satin column’s or a fill’s subpaths are, and n + 1 points are spread along it at equal distances, its ends included. n is its length divided by half the spacing, rounded half to even, at least 2, and one more if that is odd. So the zigzag has a whole number of zigs and zags, and it starts and ends on the same side.
  2. Directions. Point 0’s direction is the line to point 1, and the last point’s the line from the one before it. Every other point’s direction is halfway between the line from the point before it and the line to the point after it, each taken with length 1, so a point at a corner leans evenly into both of its sides. The lines are those between the spread points, not the path’s own.
  3. Across. Each point moves square to its direction, turned by zigzag_angle (positive turns clockwise as the drawing shows it). The points numbered 0, 2, 4 and on move to the right of the way the path goes, by half the width plus the second pull compensation. Points 1, 3, 5 and on move to the left, by half the width plus the first. A pull compensation below minus half the width moves its points over to the other side. Each point whose direction has no length stays where it is, as on a turn straight back.
  4. Closed paths. A stroke closes when one of its subpaths moves and the others, if any, draw nothing: single points, or segments of no length. That subpath’s ends must lie nearer each other than 0.05 CSS pixels. Its last needle point then moves onto its first, and the zigzag ends where it started. Ink/Stitch counts a subpath of no length as a second line, and keeps such a zigzag open (DEV-RUN-008).
  5. Repeats and bean stitch. Repeats sew the zigzag again, every other pass backwards, and each pass after the first starts with the needle point after the one the last pass ended at. Bean stitch then sews each stitch 2b + 1 times, with its list taken one after another along the stitches of all the passes, as for a running stitch (repeats and bean stitch). Ink/Stitch does not list the point a pass turns at twice in a zigzag, as it does in a running stitch, so that point takes no step of the bean list here.

The needle points come from arithmetic, square roots and the cosine and sine of the angle (stitchcraft_core::math) alone, and every platform places them alike.

Compared with Ink/Stitch. Ink/Stitch spreads the points along a running stitch it sews first, at half each spacing and within running_stitch_tolerance_mm of the path. StitchCraft spreads them along the path, flattened as Ink/Stitch flattens it for that running stitch (DEV-RUN-007). At the default spacing those running stitches are 0.2 mm long, chords of the path’s curves, so the two lines and the points spread along them differ by hundredths of a millimetre. A part whose length lies close to a rounding boundary of its count of zigs and zags may have one zig and zag more or fewer. With several spacings, Ink/Stitch’s zigzag takes the first, and its running stitch takes half of each, one after another. A long one cuts across a curve by up to running_stitch_tolerance_mm, and the zigzag follows it there, inside the path. StitchCraft sews the first spacing along the path. A part of the path that is one point is not stitched (SC-W0401), as for a running stitch (DEV-RUN-004).

Properties (conformance)

  • The needle points of each part, before repeats, are n + 1 with n even, as step 1 counts them, and their places along the flattened path are L / n apart (REQ-RUN-009).
  • Each needle point lies across its direction from its place on the path, turned by zigzag_angle: even points at half the width plus the second pull compensation to the right, odd points at half the width plus the first to the left (REQ-RUN-010).
  • A closed stroke ends on its first needle point (REQ-RUN-011).
  • Repeats and bean stitch sew the zigzag as step 5 says, with no step of the bean list for a turn (REQ-RUN-012).

Diagnostics

SC-W0401 for a part of the path that is one point. Finishing leaves out a needle point of each stitch shorter than the shortest stitch (SC-I0504). At the default spacing, 0.4 mm, and a shortest stitch of 0.3 mm, that is every stitch of a straight zigzag narrower than 0.224 mm, and the zigzag sews as a line. SC-W0405 names a zigzag with stitches that short, and the width it needs. It counts the stitches that are sewn. A closed zigzag’s last needle point moves onto its first before they are counted. That stitch can be the only short one, as at a teardrop’s tip. Ink/Stitch’s shortest stitch is 0.1 mm by default, and it sews such a zigzag as it is.

Ripple stitch (M10.8, M10.11)

A ripple stitch sews lines that spread out from the stroke: copies of its line that shrink towards a point or follow a guide, or lines along a satin column from one of its rails to the other. They are sewn as one running stitch, and a grid’s lines and a clip’s parts as runs of their own. It suits light textures that fade to a point, such as a shell. Its lines may cross. StitchCraft sews it as Ink/Stitch sews its ripple stitch (stroke_method ripple_stitch), read at d59c9ab (lib/stitches/ripple_stitch.py, the ripple settings in lib/elements/stroke.py, and how it finds its helpers in lib/marker.py).

Parameters: line_count, min_line_dist_mm, exponent, flip_exponent, skip_start, skip_end, flip_copies, join_style, staggers, reverse and manual_pattern_placement, the rails’ reverse_rails and swap_satin_rails, a guide’s satin_guide_pattern_position, scale_axis, scale_start, scale_end and rotate_ripples, and the grid’s grid_size_mm and grid_first (RippleParams). Each line is sewn with the running stitch’s settings, its length and tolerance, random length and random_seed, and the whole ripple with repeats and bean_stitch_repeats.

The stroke’s lines

The stroke’s subpaths are flattened as a shape’s outline is, within a tenth of a CSS pixel, and each one that moves is a line. A subpath that is one point is not stitched (SC-W0401). Lines shorter together than the shortest stitch sew nothing, and SC-W0401 gives their length. The lines say which ripple the stroke sews:

  • 2 lines or more are a satin column’s rails and rungs, read by the satin column’s own code (satin), and the ripple runs along the column, its lines between the rails.
  • 1 open line ripples towards a target point, or along its guide (guided ripples).
  • 1 closed line, whose ends lie within 0.05 CSS pixels of each other, spirals in to the target point, or along its guide.

The target point

Ink/Stitch’s target_point command tied to the stroke gives the target point, read as the start and end commands are read (SVG input). In a VectorCraft document a path named stitch:target grouped with the stroke gives it, at the middle of the box round its anchors (VectorCraft integration). Without either, the target point is the centroid of the convex hull of the stroke’s lines, as GEOS finds a polygon’s centroid. Where the lines’ points all lie in a line, the hull is the segment between the 2 farthest apart, and the target point its middle. One point is its own hull.

Places

Each line has a place t, from 0 to 1. Of n lines, line k is at (k / (n − 1)) raised to the power exponent, which is at least 0.1. With flip_exponent, line k is at 1 less the place of line n − 1 − k. Above 1, the exponent packs the lines together towards the first line, and below 1 towards the last. With 1 line, its place is 0, or 1 with flip_exponent.

  • Along a column. Pairs of points are placed across the column every running_stitch_tolerance_mm, as a satin column places its pairs (satin), with no compensation and nothing at random. The rails are read as a satin column reads its own, with the ripple’s reverse_rails and swap_satin_rails and the satin column’s defaults for its other settings, and cut into sections at their rungs (REQ-RIP-018). With reverse_rails automatic, the second rail is turned where it runs against the first. Line k goes through the point t of the way from each pair’s point on the first rail to its point on the second. The first line lies along the first rail and the last along the second.
  • Towards a target point. The line is first sewn as a running stitch with the stroke’s stitch lengths and tolerance, without random length (placement). Its needle points are the outline, a grid size apart with grid_size_mm (the grid). With manual_pattern_placement the outline is the first subpath’s nodes instead, and its curves’ control points are left out. Line k goes through the point t of the way from each point of the outline to the target point: the outline shrunk towards the target point by 1 − t. The first line is the outline itself, and a line at t 1 is the target point alone. Another line reaches the target point only where the outline does.

How many lines

  1. line_count gives the count. With min_line_dist_mm, the count is the shortest length among the column’s pairs, or among the lines from the outline’s points to the target point, divided by min_line_dist_mm and rounded up. The count then keeps the parity of line_count, odd or even, by being 1 less where it differs, so that the ripple ends on the same side however large the stroke is drawn. It is at least 1, and at most 1,000, or 999 where 1,000 would change its parity. SC-W0407 says when it is cut, where Ink/Stitch sews every line (DEV-RIP-002). line_count itself stops at 1,000. A pair or a line of length 0 counts as Ink/Stitch counts it: the shortest is taken from those after it, and is 0 when it is the last. So a column whose rails meet at its end takes the least count, whatever min_line_dist_mm says.
  2. A closed line and the point join (join_style 1) add 1 line, since bending makes one line of 2.
  3. A ripple along a column has at least 2 lines.

Guided ripples

A ripple of 1 line can follow a guide instead of shrinking towards its target point (REQ-RIP-012 to REQ-RIP-014). In an SVG file, a path in the stroke’s group with Ink/Stitch’s guide-line or anchor-line start marker is a helper (SVG input). In a VectorCraft document, a path in the stroke’s group named stitch:guide or stitch:anchor is one (VectorCraft integration). Each copy is the outline moved, then turned about a point and scaled about it along x and y, in that order. A copy’s points keep the outline’s order, so point j of every copy lies on one line across the ripple, the line a grid sews. Copies are counted as lines are (how many lines), and bend and are sewn as lines are.

Along a guide line

A guide of 1 subpath is a guide line, flattened as a shape’s outline is, or taken through its nodes where it is a manual stitch. Unless manual_pattern_placement is on, the outline is placed again along itself at the same stitch lengths, as Ink/Stitch places it.

  • Count and places. The copies are counted along the guide’s length, and lie at the places exponent and flip_exponent give along it. With satin_guide_pattern_position render_at_rungs, a copy lies at each point of the guide instead. A guide of more than 1,000 points sews 1,000 copies, and SC-W0407 says so (DEV-RIP-002).
  • Moving. Each copy moves by the way from the guide’s start to its place.
  • Turning. With rotate_ripples, the default, each copy after the first turns about its place by the angle from the guide’s direction at its start to the direction from the copy before’s place. The start’s direction is taken over the guide’s first 0.1 CSS pixels.
  • Scaling. Copy i of n is scaled about its place by an even step from scale_start to scale_end percent. scale_axis 1 scales along x alone and 2 along y alone. 3 scales nothing, and 0, the default, scales along both axes. A file without scale_end sets 0, and the copies shrink to the guide’s end.

Between a satin guide’s rails

A guide that is a satin column, its satin_column on, or that has several subpaths, is a satin guide. Its rails are read with its own settings, but the ripple’s reverse_rails and swap_satin_rails, and cut at its rungs. Each copy lies on a pair of points across the guide, from its first rail to its second. It moves to the pair’s first point, and turns and scales about it so that the outline’s ends lie on the pair’s points.

  • Line count, satin_guide_pattern_position’s default. The pairs lie at an even spacing, as a satin column places its pairs. Their count comes from the length of the guide’s middle, as it comes from a guide line’s length. The middle is the line through the middles of the guide’s pairs at its own zigzag spacing.
  • At the rungs (render_at_rungs). A pair lies at the end of each section.
  • Adaptive. Pairs are tried every 0.1 CSS pixels. The outline, turned so that the line between its ends runs along x, has a box round it. A copy is kept on a pair where its box clears the last kept copy’s box moved across the guide by min_line_dist_mm, or by 0.1 CSS pixels without it. Past 1,000 such places, the copies are 1,000 (SC-W0407). As in Ink/Stitch, the box moves to the left of the line from the pair’s first point to its second, in the drawing’s own coordinates, whichever way the guide runs. Where it runs with its first rail on the left, each copy clears the last by min_line_dist_mm. The other way, their boxes overlap by as much.

A guide whose rails close places its first pair again at its end, and that copy is left out, as is the only pair of a guide too short for 2. Between a satin guide’s rails, exponent, flip_exponent, scale_start and scale_end place nothing.

A closed line with no anchor line, its ends within 0.05 CSS pixels, is placed otherwise (DEV-RIP-005). Each copy is centred on its pair’s middle by the outline’s centroid, as GEOS finds a line’s. It is scaled by its pair’s width over the first pair’s, on scale_axis, and with rotate_ripples it turns as its pair turns from the first. At the rungs, the copies lie at every section’s end but the guide’s own end.

The anchor line

An anchor line says which points of the outline lie on a satin guide’s pairs: the start and the end of its first subpath, in place of the outline’s ends. A closed line with an anchor line is placed so, and not centred. The copies along a guide line don’t use it. One whose ends are one point is left out with SC-W0409.

A guide that can’t place copies

A guide line of no length can’t place copies. A satin guide can’t place them when its rails don’t make a section, and it can’t when its middle has no length or its first pair has no width. SC-W0409 says why, and the ripple sews as it would without the guide (DEV-RIP-004). A satin guide whose own settings don’t read is left out so too. What reading a satin guide warns of is said, but not its errors, which would refuse the ripple: SC-W0409 says why the guide is left out instead.

Bent lines

A closed line’s lines and the point join’s bend into the next line. So do the lines along a column without flip_copies. At the outline’s point or the column’s pair i of m, line k lies i / (m − 1) of the way from its own point to line k + 1’s. It starts on its own start and ends on the next line’s end. The point join flips every other line, counted from the first of the count, towards a target point always and along a column with flip_copies on. Such a line lies 1 − i / (m − 1) of the way, so it starts on the next line’s start and ends on its own end. The last line bends into none and is left out, and the bent lines are 1 fewer than the count. A closed line’s lines, bent, run on from one to the next in a spiral that ends at the target point.

Skipping lines

skip_start leaves out the first lines and skip_end the last. Ink/Stitch leaves out none where they add up to the number of lines or more, and so does StitchCraft. SC-W0408 says so.

Sewing the lines

One line at a time

Lines along a column or towards a target point with flip_copies on, the default, are sewn one at a time, each as a running stitch of its own with the stroke’s stitch lengths and tolerance. A line of 1 point, from a column of 1 pair, is left out.

  • Flat join (join_style 0, the default). Every other line, counted from the first sewn, is sewn from its end. The ripple goes back and forth. A straight running stitch joins each line’s last needle point to the next line’s first.
  • Point join (join_style 1). The lines are bent, and every other line, counted from the first of the count, is sewn from its end. Each line then starts where the one before ended, and nothing joins them.
  • Staggers. With staggers 0, a line is placed from the end it is sewn from, and a line of no length sews its one point. With any other value, line i, counted from the first sewn, is placed from its start, ((i / staggers) mod 1) of the sum of the stitch lengths before it, so that the needle points of lines side by side don’t line up. The line is lengthened back along its first segment by that much, or in proportion less where the segment is shorter than 0.1 CSS pixels, and sewn as a running stitch. The needle points before the line’s start are left out, and the start is the first needle point. A needle point nearer the start than the shortest stitch is left out too. The line is then sewn from its end where it turns back. A line of no length sews its one point, but nothing at a phase of 0, as Ink/Stitch at d59c9ab sews it when run as a black box. staggers is 1 when a file sets none. Each line is then placed from its start. Ink/Stitch’s settings window shows 0.
  • With random length, each line draws from its own stream of the element’s generator, keyed by its place among the lines sewn.

One run

A closed line’s lines, and the others with flip_copies off, are sewn as one running stitch through all their points in order. Each line is sewn from its start, and the needle goes straight from one line’s end to the next one’s start.

Through their own points

With manual_pattern_placement, each line’s points are its needle points (REQ-RIP-016). With flip_copies, every other line is sewn from its end, the first among them. A straight run joins each line to the next, cut into the fewest even stitches no longer than the first stitch length.

The whole ripple

reverse sews the whole ripple backwards, from its last needle point. repeats sews each of its runs there and back, and bean_stitch_repeats then sews each run as a running stitch’s (repeats and bean stitch). A line sewn one at a time that starts where the one before ended lists that needle point again, as Ink/Stitch lists it, and it takes a step of the bean list each time. So does a run’s turn, where a pass after the first starts. A ripple starts and ends where its stitches do, whatever its neighbours. It offers the element before it its first subpath’s start, as every stroke does. Lines near the target point can be shorter than the shortest stitch. Finalize then leaves out their needle points that lie too near the one before (finalize).

The lines’ points come from arithmetic, square roots and powers (stitchcraft_core::math) alone, and every platform places them alike. Each point of each line costs a unit of work, and the points must fit in the stitches the budget has left before they are made.

The grid

With grid_size_mm, the ripple sews a grid too (REQ-RIP-015). Grid line j runs across the ripple’s own lines, through point j of every line left after the skips. The outline’s needle points, or a column’s pairs, lie a grid size apart, raised to twice the shortest stitch as a stitch length is (SC-W0402, DEV-RUN-003). Without flip_copies, where the lines run one way and join flat, every other grid line runs the other way. The grid then starts at its corner nearest the ripple’s last needle point, the first of them when several are as near: its first or its last line, from its start or its end. It is sewn as the lines are sewn one at a time, every other one from its end only with flip_copies, as a run of its own after the ripple’s. Its run is not repeated. grid_first sews every run backwards and in the other order, so that the grid comes first.

Clips

A ripple cut to clips sews the pieces of its needle points that lie inside them (REQ-RIP-017), cut where they cross the edges of the clips’ area as a stroke’s line is cut. Its lines cross those edges again and again, so the pieces are many and short. A part of the area routes its pieces as a tatami fill routes its rows (routing), sewing each piece once as it lies. Between pieces the needle goes along the part’s outline, never under them, from the outline’s point nearest the first piece’s start to its point nearest the last piece’s end. Every part is a run of its own. The parts come in the order of their first pieces along the ripple, and the part of the last piece comes last. The travel before a part’s first piece and after its last is left out. With a grid, the ripple’s runs keep the travel after their last piece, and the grid’s runs the travel before their first. The needle then goes along the outline from the ripple’s end to the grid. Runs are repeated after that. A ripple wholly outside its clips isn’t sewn (SC-W0013, DEV-RIP-003).

Not applied

Ink/Stitch reads a column’s rails with the element’s push_compensation_mm, which StitchCraft leaves at 0. SC-W0406 names it when it is set to anything else.

Compared with Ink/Stitch

The lines, their places and their count are Ink/Stitch’s. The running stitches along each line, along the joins and along the outline that a ripple towards a target point is made from place their needle points as StitchCraft’s running stitch does (DEV-RUN-001 to DEV-RUN-005). So the outline’s points differ a little, and the copies made from them. With min_line_dist_mm, the count towards a target point is measured from those points, and can differ by 2 where the shortest length falls near a whole number of min_line_dist_mm. Ink/Stitch sews nothing of lines shorter together than 0.1 CSS pixels, and StitchCraft nothing of lines shorter together than the shortest stitch (DEV-RUN-004).

A guided ripple’s copies are made of the outline’s running stitch too, and differ as it does. Drawn with straight sides, TS-17C’s guided wave and circle sew the same needle points in both programs, their locks aside. StitchCraft sews otherwise on purpose where a guide can’t place copies, where a ripple lies wholly outside its clips, and where a closed line’s ends almost meet (DEV-RIP-003 to DEV-RIP-005).

Ink/Stitch starts a staggered line where its stitches have run the phase from the stretch’s start, measured along them. Where the stretch is the whole phase, that is the line’s start, or near it where the stitches cut a bend. Where the first segment is shorter than 0.1 CSS pixels, the stretch is shorter than the phase, and Ink/Stitch leaves the rest of the phase of the line unsewn. StitchCraft starts every line at its start (DEV-RIP-001).

Properties (conformance)

  • A stroke of 2 lines or more ripples along the column its rails and rungs make, a stroke of 1 open line towards its target point, and a stroke of 1 closed line in a spiral to it (REQ-RIP-001).
  • The target point is the stroke’s target_point command or stitch:target marker, or else the centroid of the convex hull of its lines (REQ-RIP-002).
  • The count is line_count, or what min_line_dist_mm gives with the parity of line_count kept, 1 more for a closed line or the point join, at least 2 along a column and at most 1,000 (REQ-RIP-003).
  • Line k lies at its place, (k / (n − 1))^exponent, or flipped, between the rails or towards the target point. A copy of the outline reaches the target point only at place 1, or where the outline does (REQ-RIP-004).
  • skip_start and skip_end leave out lines at each end, and the ripple keeps every line when together they would leave out all of them (REQ-RIP-005).
  • With flip_copies, the lines are sewn back and forth, joined flat by straight running stitches or at a point. Without it, and for a closed line, they are sewn as one running stitch (REQ-RIP-006).
  • Each line’s stitches keep within the running stitch’s tolerance of the line through its points (REQ-RIP-007).
  • Staggered lines start their needle points ((i / staggers) mod 1) of the stitch lengths’ sum before their start. A line of no length sews its one point, but nothing when staggered at a phase of 0 (REQ-RIP-008).
  • reverse sews the ripple backwards, then repeats and bean_stitch_repeats sew it as a running stitch’s (REQ-RIP-009).
  • push_compensation_mm along a column is named and not applied (REQ-RIP-010).
  • TS-17C’s ripples sew in Ink/Stitch as in StitchCraft (REQ-RIP-011).
  • Copies follow a guide line, moved, turned with it and scaled from scale_start to scale_end (REQ-RIP-012).
  • A satin guide places each copy’s ends on a pair across it, or centres a closed line on each pair (REQ-RIP-013), and an anchor line’s ends take the place of the outline’s (REQ-RIP-014).
  • A grid’s lines join the same point of every line, start nearest the ripple’s end and sew as a run of their own, first with grid_first (REQ-RIP-015).
  • With manual_pattern_placement, the lines are sewn through their own points (REQ-RIP-016).
  • Clips cut the ripple’s needle points, and each part of their area routes its pieces (REQ-RIP-017).
  • A column and a satin guide read their rails with the ripple’s reverse_rails and swap_satin_rails (REQ-RIP-018).
  • TS-17C’s guided ripples sew in Ink/Stitch as in StitchCraft (REQ-RIP-019).

Diagnostics

SC-W0401 for a subpath that is one point and for lines too short for the shortest stitch, SC-W0406 for push_compensation_mm along a column, SC-W0407 for a count or a guide’s places cut to 1,000, SC-W0408 for skips that would leave out every line, and SC-W0409 for a guide or an anchor line that can’t place copies. SC-W0013 says when the ripple lies wholly outside its clips, and SC-W0402 when the grid size is raised. A ripple along a column says what the satin column’s reading of its rails and rungs says (SC-W0202, SC-W0203, SC-W0205, SC-W0207, SC-W0210), and SC-E0216 when no section of the column has length.
