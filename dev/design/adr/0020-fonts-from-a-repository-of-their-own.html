StitchCraft

ADR-0020: Embroidery fonts come from a repository of their own, under licences that allow shared adaptations

Status: Accepted · 2026-11-08, after spike M14.1 · amends decision 4 of ADR-0001

Context

M14 sews text in embroidery fonts, folders of glyphs digitized by hand in Ink/Stitch’s format (fonts). The fonts are StitchCraft’s own, those of Ink/Stitch’s library whose licence allows it, and those a user has installed for Ink/Stitch. Decision 4 of ADR-0001 says that fonts are never copied, so no font file enters this repository. Spike M14.1 chooses where StitchCraft’s fonts live and which licences they may be under. It also chooses the reader of the format, which M14.2’s lettering builds on.

What was measured or read, on 2026-11-08:

  • Ink/Stitch’s fonts at c7e3a05. The 131 fonts whose licence file the corpus takes hold 302 MB of files, 247 MB of them glyph files, and 59 MB as a gzipped tar archive. One font’s kerning table holds 196,633 pairs. Ink/Stitch’s own packages compress every font file with xz (bin/compress-fonts at d59c9ab).
  • VectorCraft’s fonts are in storytold/craft-fonts. Its fonts/manifest.txt gives each font file a row of its family, style, file, scripts, licence, licence file, SHA-256 and source, and its ATTRIBUTION.md credits each font. VectorCraft’s build embeds the fonts of the checkout that the build option CRAFT_FONTS_DIR names, and none without it. Release jobs check craft-fonts out at a pinned commit, and release packages include each embedded font’s licence file. Tests that need the fonts skip with a message without them. The web build embeds 1 font of 4.7 MB, so that its wasm stays under a static host’s limit of 25 MiB (VectorCraft’s docs/development.md and crates/text/build.rs at 3f8d7a0).
  • The corpus of M8.6 pins Ink/Stitch’s font repository by a commit and the SHA-256 of the archive GitHub makes of it, and cargo xtask corpus fetches it into a cache outside the repository (the corpus).
  • The licence files of the 131 fonts the corpus takes name an allowed licence first. 32 of them also point at the terms of the font each was made from, or at a permission its author gave (licences in the font repository).
  • Ink/Stitch’s lettering, read at d59c9ab and run as a black box, gives each key of font.json a default. It finds the glyph files of each direction and measures each glyph’s box and its file’s baseline (the reader).

Not read: craftrules’ standards/fonts.md, the rules craft-fonts follows. Its page wasn’t fetched on 2026-11-08. The decision below rests on what VectorCraft’s own files say of craft-fonts, and the owner checks it against the standard.

Decision

1. The font repository

  1. A repository of their own, KaboomFox/stitchcraft-fonts, owned with this repository by its owner, who creates it. Each font is a folder under fonts/ in Ink/Stitch’s format, with its licence file named LICENSE, as Ink/Stitch reads it. A row of fonts/manifest.toml describes each font, as ASSETS.md describes an asset:

    • its folder and its name
    • its author, its source and the commit it was taken at
    • its licence and licence file
    • the credit its licence requires, and what was changed
    • who checked its licence, and when

    ATTRIBUTION.md lists the credits.

  2. What it holds. StitchCraft’s own fonts, and the fonts of inkstitch/embroidery-fonts whose licence it takes (decision 2), copied whole from a commit the manifest records. Ported fonts join after a sew-out, when the owner decides so (fonts).

  3. Pinned by fonts.toml at this repository’s root, by its commit and the SHA-256 of GitHub’s archive of the commit, as conformance/inkstitch/fonts.toml pins the corpus. A new xtask command, fonts, fetches the archive into the cache, checks its sum and unpacks it there, with the corpus’s code. Moving the pin is a pull request, whose CI reads every font of the new commit.

  4. stitch finds a font by its folder’s name or by its name (M14.2). It looks in each folder that --fonts names, then in STITCHCRAFT_FONTS, then in stitchcraft/fonts in the user’s data folder. The folders where Ink/Stitch keeps a user’s fonts come last. Each release publishes the pinned repository’s fonts as an archive beside the binaries, for that data folder, and no binary holds them.

  5. The VectorCraft plug-in doesn’t read files. Its build embeds the fonts that a list in its crate names, from the checkout that the build option STITCHCRAFT_FONTS_DIR names, as VectorCraft’s build embeds craft-fonts. Without the option it embeds none. M14.4 chooses the list within the plug-in’s size.

  6. Without the repository, everything builds and tests. No font file is committed to this repository, and none is needed. Tests draw their fonts in code. A case of a real font reads it from the cache, and says it is skipped where the cache doesn’t have it. Every CI job that runs cargo xtask ci fetches the corpus, and will fetch the repository once it is pinned.

2. The licences it takes

  1. Taken: the SIL Open Font License and CC0 or the public domain, and CC BY and CC BY-SA. A font’s adaptations under each may be shared for any use. Licences that forbid commercial use or adaptations are left out, and so is the GPL. A font’s own terms are left out too, as are terms that are unclear.
  2. A font’s licence is the one its licence file names first, by the corpus’s rule (stitchcraft_svg::font::licence). The font_license of font.json is a label. Where it names another licence the file counts, the manifest row says so, and the font’s author hears of it.
  3. Checked when a font joins. The repository’s CI reads every font with StitchCraft’s reader. It fails when a font doesn’t read or has no licence file, when the licence is not one of those taken, or when the manifest row names another. Then a person reads the whole licence file, and the licence of the font it was made from. For the OFL that includes the Reserved Font Names, which a modified font may not use. The row records who checked it and when, and the owner approves each pull request that adds a font.
  4. An ambiguous licence file keeps its font out. The file is ambiguous when the rule doesn’t find a licence that is taken in it, or when the font rests on a permission given for Ink/Stitch alone, or on the terms of the font it was made from that don’t say adaptations may be shared. The manifest lists such a font as left out, with why, until its author makes the terms clear. At c7e3a05 that leaves out 7 fonts whose original’s author allowed its use for Ink/Stitch, and a person reads 10 more before they join (licences in the font repository).
  5. A CC BY or CC BY-SA font’s credit reaches the user in 4 places. The manifest row and ATTRIBUTION.md give it, with the licence’s address and the changes. The release’s font archive includes both files and every licence file. stitch prints the credit whenever it sets text in such a font, and lists it beside the font (M14.2). The plug-in shows it with the font’s name in VectorCraft (M14.4). StitchCraft says the licence and the credit, and a user who shares a design made with the font follows the licence.

3. The reader

  1. In stitchcraft-svg, as stitchcraft_svg::font::read. A glyph file is an Ink/Stitch SVG file, and each glyph’s design is read by the SVG reader. Its box and its file’s baseline are measured with the SVG reader’s path data, transforms and page, which no other crate has. The reader takes a folder’s files as bytes. The plug-in can then read fonts built into it, and stitch the files on disk.

  2. What it reads, as the reader describes it:

    • every key of font.json that Ink/Stitch reads, with Ink/Stitch’s default, as the font settings page gives them
    • the glyph files of each direction, and each glyph’s name, label and design
    • each glyph’s box and its file’s baseline, as Ink/Stitch’s lettering measures them
    • the licence file, and the licence it names

    It also reads what lettering needs of the keys, such as a pair’s kerning with an older font’s pairs and a glyph’s advance with its defaults, and the heights the font sews at and its default direction.

  3. Bounded and never crashing. One meter of the budget counts the whole font, and a font that needs more is refused with SC-E0004 (REQ-FONT-007). Property tests read malformed fonts.

  4. Coded diagnostics for what it can’t read. SC-E1101 refuses a font it can’t read. SC-W1102 names a setting read at its default, and SC-W1103 a compressed file left out. SC-W1104 names a glyph left out, as Ink/Stitch’s lettering leaves it out. SC-I1105 says where the font is read as Ink/Stitch reads it, in a way its author may not expect.

  5. The line with M14.2. M14.1 reads a font into its settings and glyphs, and what each key means. M14.2 sets text. It matches characters to glyphs, with ligatures, Arabic forms, the letter case and the default glyph, and places them by advances, kerning and the spacings the user sets. It aligns the lines and keeps the scale within the font’s heights, and auto-satin routes the text. M14.2 also chooses the crate of lettering, and how it places the glyphs of the 3 fonts whose box StitchCraft offsets from Ink/Stitch’s (the reader).

Consequences

  • ADR-0001’s decision 4 gains the amendment, in a note that links here.
  • The repository, its manifest’s check, fonts.toml, the xtask command that fetches it and the release’s font archive come once the owner has created the repository, with M14.2 or a step she names. Until then the cases of a real font read the corpus.
  • M15.2’s crate, planned as stitchcraft-fonts, takes another name, stitchcraft-outlines, so that one name means one thing.
  • The corpus, 69 MB, is fetched in 3 more CI jobs, and the Actions cache keeps it under the pin’s hash.

Alternatives considered

  • Fonts in storytold/craft-fonts, beside VectorCraft’s. Rejected: craft-fonts is for the font files that the apps build in, with a row for each face and its scripts. Embroidery fonts are folders of digitized designs, 302 MB of them, read and checked otherwise, and craft-fonts belongs to VectorCraft’s maintainers. The fonts can move there if StitchCraft moves into VectorCraft (ADR-0011).
  • A fork of inkstitch/embroidery-fonts. Rejected: it includes the 11 fonts StitchCraft can’t take, with their GPL and non-commercial licence files and their history.
  • Fonts in this repository. Rejected by ADR-0001’s decision 4 and by VectorCraft’s rule that no font file is committed. The clean-room check would also find the GPL’s text in a licence file.
  • stitch fetching Ink/Stitch’s repository at the corpus’s pin, and reading the fonts the licence rule takes. Rejected: it has no place for StitchCraft’s own fonts or for corrections, and every user would download the 11 fonts StitchCraft can’t take. stitch would also need the network.
  • Every font built into stitch and the plug-in. Rejected: 302 MB of glyph files, where VectorCraft’s web build keeps its wasm under 25 MiB.
  • The OFL alone. Rejected: 29 of the 131 fonts are in the public domain or under CC BY or CC BY-SA. Their adaptations may be shared for any use too.
  • Non-commercial licences as well. Rejected: StitchCraft can’t check how a design is used, and a user who sells embroidery couldn’t use them. The roadmap rules them out.
  • font_license as the font’s licence. Rejected: it disagrees with the licence file for 3 fonts, and the file grants the rights.
  • The reader in a crate of its own, above stitchcraft-svg. Rejected for M14.1: the box and the baseline need the SVG reader’s private path data, transforms and page. Lettering may have a crate of its own (M14.2).
  • Each glyph file read once into one design, its elements sorted by glyph. Rejected: the SVG reader would have to keep each element’s glyph, and the glyph differential cuts each glyph out anyway. Cutting gives each glyph the design the differential sews in both programs.
