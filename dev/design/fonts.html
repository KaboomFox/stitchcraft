StitchCraft

Fonts

Lettering sews text in a font. M14 sets text in embroidery fonts, digitized glyph by glyph in Ink/Stitch’s font format. M15 ports an ordinary TrueType or OpenType font automatically. It reads each glyph’s outline at the height the text is set and works out how the glyph sews (roadmap). This page records the research both milestones rest on, counted and read on 2026-11-02. The sources are listed at the end. Since M8.6, Ink/Stitch’s fonts are a corpus of the differential, each glyph sewn in both programs (the corpus).

Ink/Stitch’s fonts

Ink/Stitch’s fonts are a repository of their own, inkstitch/embroidery-fonts, which Ink/Stitch includes as its fonts folder. At commit c7e3a05 it has 142 fonts. Ink/Stitch’s tutorial on making a font and its page on the font tools document the format. The numbers below were counted from the font files.

  • A font is a folder. font.json gives the font’s settings, and a glyph file gives its glyphs for each direction it sews in. 137 fonts sew left to right (ltr.svg) and 17 right to left (rtl.svg), 12 of them both ways. One font also sews top to bottom and bottom to top. 5 large fonts split a direction into several files, in a folder of that name. The folder also has a licence file and a preview picture.
  • A glyph file has a layer for each glyph, labelled GlyphLayer- followed by the character, and a guide named baseline. A layer can be named for several characters, as for a ligature. An Arabic letter has a layer for each of its forms.
  • The settings name the font, its licence and the font it was made from, with that font’s address. size is the height of a reference capital in millimetres, and min_scale and max_scale bound the heights the font sews at. horiz_adv_x gives each glyph’s advance, with a default and the space’s. kerning_pairs gives each pair’s adjustment, which is subtracted from the advance. Further keys force upper or lower case, name the glyph sewn for a missing one, and say whether the font sews lines back and forth, sorts its colours or is routed by auto-satin.
  • Heights. A font sews from size × min_scale to size × max_scale. The least heights range from 2.8 mm (Glacial Tiny) to 144 mm and the greatest from 6.9 to 468 mm. Their medians are 20 and 45 mm. 21 fonts are tagged tiny.
  • Stitch types. 94 fonts sew satin columns, 73,422 of them. 119 fonts sew running stitches, 67 tatami fills and 25 cross stitch.

How they are made. 134 of the fonts name the font they were made from, 36 of them from Google Fonts. The tutorial starts from a TrueType or OpenType file. FontForge writes it as an SVG font, and an Ink/Stitch tool splits that into glyph layers at a chosen height. Each glyph is then digitized by hand, with its satin columns and rungs, where it starts and ends, its trims and its settings. The tutorial calls each letter “a small embroidery in itself” and ends with “real stitchouts are the ultimate test”.

Small sizes are separate fonts. Pacificlo tiny sews Pacifico from 4.2 to 9.4 mm, and Pacificlo from 13.6 to 23.8 mm. Pacificlo tiny, Amitaclo small and Chicken Little KOR Small keep their larger fonts’ columns with lighter settings. Their satin spacing is 0.25 mm, without a zigzag underlay, and their pull compensation is 0.05 to 0.1 mm in place of 0.2 to 0.3 mm. Venezia small also sews satin columns where Venezia sews running stitches. At their least heights, the median satin column of 4 small fonts is 0.47 to 0.61 mm wide, and a tenth of their columns are narrower than 0.3 to 0.5 mm. The 4 are Ink/Stitch Small Font, Glacial Tiny, Pacificlo tiny and Venezia small. Automatic digitizing sews a line narrower than 1 mm (min_satin_stroke_width_mm) as a run, and compensates every satin column by 0.4 mm.

Licences. By their licence files, 102 fonts are under the OFL, 25 under CC BY-SA, 1 under CC BY, and 3 in the public domain or under CC0. 8 are under CC BY-NC-SA, 1 under CC Attribution-NoDerivatives and 1 under the GPL-3.0. NickAinley, the 142nd, is under a free font’s licence, with its authors’ permission for Ink/Stitch. The licence field of font.json disagrees with the licence file for 3 fonts. It names the OFL for precious, whose licence file is the GPL-3.0 with a ban on selling the font. It names CC BY-SA for cogs_KOR, under CC BY, and for nick_ainley. Ink/Stitch 3.3.0 renamed 14 fonts to respect the OFL’s Reserved Font Names.

Lettering. Ink/Stitch’s lettering (lib/lettering at d59c9ab) advances each glyph by its own advance, less the kerning of its pair, and each word by the space’s advance. The user can widen the spacing of letters, of words and of lines by a number of millimetres. Lines align left, centre or right, or are justified as a block. A trim can follow each letter, word or line. Lines sew back and forth in a font with a variant for the other direction, and a sortable font’s colours sew in order. Batch lettering clamps the scale to the font’s range. In a font whose auto_satin is on, Auto-Route Satin Columns routes the satin columns and strokes of the whole text, their order kept and without trims, before the trims are added and the colours sorted. Each piece goes to the end of the group its element was in. The key is on when font.json leaves it out, and at c7e3a05 every font sets it, Ink/Stitch Small Font and Medium Font on and the other 140 off. StitchCraft’s lettering routes such a font’s text with M10.4’s function (routing tools).

The corpus

M8.6 makes Ink/Stitch’s fonts a corpus of the differential. cargo xtask corpus fetches the archive GitHub makes of c7e3a05, the commit conformance/inkstitch/fonts.toml pins, and checks its SHA-256 before it unpacks it. The fonts stay in a cache outside the repository: the folder STITCHCRAFT_CORPUS names, or stitchcraft/corpus in the user’s cache folder. No font file is committed. Outside the repository the gates leave them alone, where the clean-room check would find the GPL in one font’s licence. cargo clean keeps them, and every worktree shares them.

The fonts it takes. A licence file names the font’s own licence first, in a sentence such as “This font has been adapted for Ink/Stitch and is licensed under the SIL Open Font License”. It often names the licence of the font it was made from after it, or quotes it. So of the licences a file names, the first is the font’s. The corpus takes the fonts under the OFL, CC0 or the public domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA, whose adaptations may be shared for any use: 131 fonts, with 167 glyph files and 21,179 glyphs. The report names the other 11, which are never sewn.

A glyph as a design. Ink/Stitch’s lettering takes every group labelled GlyphLayer-, wherever it lies in the file, and so does the differential: of the 21,616 glyphs of the 142 fonts, 3,030 lie inside other groups. Each glyph is cut out of its glyph file as a design of its own. Its group is shown, as lettering shows it, and every other drawn element is left out, in the groups round it too. The rest of the file stays as it was written, its version and definitions with the symbols of Ink/Stitch’s commands among them, and so do the transforms and styles of the groups round the glyph. Both programs sew that file. StitchCraft plans it for the reference machine with a hoop 400 mm a side, since a glyph file is drawn at its font’s height and a glyph can be wider than the reference hoop.

The sample. Every night the differential sews 4 glyphs of each font, 524 in all, and every Sunday all 21,179, in 6 parts. A font’s glyphs are taken in the order of its files, sorted by path, and in each file in its order. The sample takes the glyphs at the places 0, n/4, n/2 and 3n/4 of n, rounded down. It spreads over each font, from its first glyph, and moves only when the pin does. Run from its checkout, Ink/Stitch logs at its debug level, and a glyph took it 10 seconds, against 1.6 with the log turned off. On 2 cores the sample then takes 12 minutes, and a twentieth of every glyph 22.

What differs. These counts are from runs on 3 November 2026, before M10.1 brought simple crosses. Of the sample’s 524 glyphs, 357 agree by every measure. 98 are of stitch types StitchCraft doesn’t sew yet, which the report lists, 66 of them in cross stitch and the rest in tartan fills, ripples and guided fills. They join the comparison with M10’s steps, the simple crosses with M10.1 and Jersey 15’s double crosses with M10.2. Sewn alone with M10.2, every one of Jersey 15’s 162 glyphs agrees, the 2 programs’ threads overlapping by 1.000 in each. Ripples join with M10.8. Of the 324 glyphs of the 3 Ondulamarif fonts, sewn alone, 144 agree and 141 differ as the ledger explains, most of them by where each program’s running stitch places the needle points of a ripple’s lines. The other 39 ripple along a guide line, which M10.11 sews. The sample’s other 69 differ as the ledger explains, conformance/inkstitch/glyphs.toml naming the glyphs each excuse is for. Trade articles on small lettering ask for capitals at least 6.4 mm tall, mixed case at least 5 mm and all capitals at least 3.8 mm (PI World). Its other minimums are 0.8 mm for a column’s width, 0.9 mm across a counter and 0.5 mm between letters. Another article puts small letters on a centre walk with pull compensation, and satin columns at least as wide as the needle. It advises against serifs and strokes of varying width under about half an inch (ASI).

Published methods. VSM’s patent US 6,934,599 (priority 2002, expired 2023) describes porting a font automatically. A glyph’s contours are grouped and cut at sharp corners and at nodes of a thinned skeleton until each part is simple. Each part sews as a straight or curved satin column or as a running stitch, its stitches square to the skeleton. The parts are ordered by how they connect. Neighbouring columns are merged, with jumps between the rest. M13’s centre lines and columns are a pipeline of that kind. Brother’s US 5,559,711 (expired 2014) chooses satin or another stitch by a region’s roundness, its area over its perimeter squared. StrokeStyles (Berio and others, 2022) splits glyphs of many scripts into overlapping strokes, from the medial axis, the outline’s convex and concave parts and 7 kinds of junction, without training data. Balashova and others (2019) fit a model of strokes to existing fonts. A thesis of 1998 at National Taiwan Normal University digitized embroidery from medial axes, a neural network classifying the junctions.

Each licence is on the allow-list of deny.toml. At the versions VectorCraft uses, skrifa, read-fonts and harfrust forbid unsafe code and build without the standard library, and VectorCraft builds them for its web app. A font file is never committed to StitchCraft. Tests build their fonts in code with write-fonts and read a corpus downloaded at pinned commits.

  • Hinting moves an outline’s points onto a screen’s pixels at small sizes, which means nothing to a needle. A port reads outlines unhinted, in font units, and scales them with StitchCraft’s own arithmetic (determinism).
  • Composite glyphs, such as most accented letters, are placed from their components. A port can digitize a base letter once for every glyph made from it.
  • Overlapping contours are common in composites and in variable fonts, where each stroke is often a contour of its own. A viewer fills a glyph by the nonzero rule, and so does the region code of M5.1. Text typed in an installed font fails in Ink/Stitch as a fill whose border crosses itself (how people use Ink/Stitch).
  • Variable fonts are set at a named instance or at axis values. A weight axis can thicken a font’s strokes at small heights, and an optical size axis gives the font’s design for small text.

Stroke widths. In Source Sans 3 Regular, a stem is 83 units at a cap height of 660 (12.6 %). At capitals 8.0 mm tall its stems are 1 mm wide, the narrowest satin column of automatic digitizing. At 4.0 mm they are 0.5 mm wide, where a run is tripled. Inter Regular’s stems are 1 mm wide at 7.8 mm, and Source Sans 3 Bold’s at 4.5 mm. Source Serif 4 Regular’s stems are 1 mm wide at 7.1 mm and its hairlines at 14.9 mm. Between those heights its letters mix satin columns and runs. A stem is 7 mm wide, where automatic digitizing fills, at capitals 56 mm tall in Source Sans 3 Regular and 31 mm tall in its Bold.

Dots. At capitals 6 mm tall, Source Sans 3 Regular’s i-dot covers 0.79 mm², its full stop 0.99 mm² and each dot of an ä 0.65 mm². Inter Regular’s i-dot covers 0.76 mm². M13 takes a part under 1 mm² for a speck, and merges it into a neighbour or leaves it out.

Licences of ported fonts

A font ported into an embroidery font is a modified version of the font. A design sewn in it is a document made with the font.

  • OFL. A “Modified Version” includes one made “by changing formats or by porting the Font Software to a new environment”, and the licence’s site counts a conversion to SVG as one. It is distributed under the OFL only, never sold by itself, and named without the font’s Reserved Font Names. “The requirement for fonts to remain under this license does not apply to any document created using the Font Software.”
  • UFL 1.0, the Ubuntu font licence, counts a change of format as a modification too. A version changed substantially takes a new name, and another adds “derivative” to the name. The licence “does not affect any document created using the Font Software”.
  • Apache-2.0 allows modified versions with the licence, its NOTICE file and a note of the changes.
  • Creative Commons. BY and BY-SA allow adaptations with credit, under the same licence for SA. NoDerivatives doesn’t allow sharing adaptations, and NonCommercial doesn’t allow commercial use.
  • Proprietary fonts follow their licences. Rare Bird’s desktop licence doesn’t allow derivative works, and requires a supplemental licence for “embroidering or sewing machines”. KG Fonts sells an embroidery licence for embroidery font files.

A font’s name table gives its copyright notice, trademark, licence description and licence URL (name IDs 0, 7, 13 and 14), which a port can read and report. Google Fonts’ fonts are under the OFL, the UFL or Apache-2.0, in a folder for each in its repository.

Where fonts live. VectorCraft’s shared fonts are in storytold/craft-fonts, an optional build input (CRAFT_FONTS_DIR), with each font’s licence text beside it (VectorCraft’s docs/development.md at 1505384). Embroidery fonts in Ink/Stitch’s format come from a repository of their own (M14.1). A ported font is made on the user’s computer from a font the user has.

Glyph rules

M13 digitizes a traced shape by its width at the size it sews (image to embroidery). A glyph is such a shape, and M13’s rules do most of the work, from centre lines and junctions to rings and the fills of broad parts. The table lists what glyphs need beyond them, with the step of M15 that adds it.

SerifsA serif is an arm with a column of its own, or a bumpM15.6

Correcting a glyph. A ported font can be written in Ink/Stitch’s format (M15.11), with its satin columns drawn with their rails and rungs as Stroke to Satin draws them. A user then corrects a glyph in its layer, in VectorCraft or in Inkscape with Ink/Stitch, and M14’s lettering sews the corrected font. VectorCraft keeps inkstitch:* attributes from SVG import to SVG export (RFC, P7). The writer puts the baselines at 0, where a reader puts a baseline when the file lacks a baseline guide. Wilcom’s converted letters are corrected the same way, as letters of the saved font.

Testing. 36 of Ink/Stitch’s fonts were digitized by hand from fonts on Google Fonts, all 36 under the OFL. The corpus pairs each original with the Ink/Stitch font made from it. A port at that font’s heights is measured against it, by the share of each glyph’s area sewn as satin columns, fills and runs, and by jumps, coverage and stitches. TS-15 sews 3 ported fonts beside the fonts digitized by hand from them, at MC-10 (machine testing).

Decisions for the owner

  • When M15 comes. The roadmap places it after M14 and before M11 and 1.0. M15.1 and M15.2 can go ahead beside M14. Moving M15 after 1.0 brings 1.0 sooner.
  • Which ported fonts are written. Text in any installed font can be sewn as a design, as Wilcom and Hatch sew it. The font’s licence is reported. Writing a ported font is making a modified version of the font. The plan writes one for a font under the OFL, the UFL, Apache-2.0, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, and for another font only when the user says they hold a licence that allows it. The owner can make that stricter or looser.
  • Ported fonts in the font repository. Ports of OFL fonts, corrected by hand and sewn out, could join StitchCraft’s font repository under new names, as Ink/Stitch’s library grew from such fonts.
  • The narrowest satin column in lettering. Automatic digitizing runs a line narrower than 1 mm, and Ink/Stitch’s small fonts sew columns about 0.5 mm wide. TS-15 sews both, and MC-10 chooses.
  • Raising P9. The RFC’s P9 proposes that VectorCraft give plug-ins type as glyph outlines, which its plans don’t cover. Without it, the exporter plug-in can’t sew type, since it can’t read font files. Whether to raise P9 on VectorCraft’s own repository, and when, is the owner’s choice.
