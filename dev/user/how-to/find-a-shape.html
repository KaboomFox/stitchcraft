StitchCraft

Find a shape by its name

A traced photo has hundreds of shapes. To mark one, guide its rows or give it an effect of its own, you first have to find it. Each shape of a design digitized automatically has a name, and the notices, the report and VectorCraft’s Layers panel all use it.

The parts of a name

A name gives how the shape sews, its number among the shapes of its thread that sew the same way, and last its thread:

  • Fill 3, Red is the third red area that sews as a fill.
  • Column 1, Deep Gold sews as satin columns, and Line 12, Black as running stitches.
  • Left out 1, White sews nothing. It is the picture’s background, or merged into the shapes beside it.

The thread is named as your machine names it. A colour that none of its threads has is named by its hex code, such as #7d6f00. The number counts down the Layers panel from its top row, so it doesn’t change when the design sews in another order.

In VectorCraft

stitch plan picture.png --traced picture.vectorcraft writes the names into the traced document. Open it in VectorCraft, and the Layers panel shows each shape by its name. To find one, type its name into the panel’s search box. Type the comma after the number: fill 1, finds Fill 1, Red and not Fill 12, Red.

Open the group’s row with its triangle to see the shapes’ rows. Here is the traced pear in the panel, as VectorCraft shows it at the panel’s default width:

At the panel’s default width a row shows about 20 characters. Longer names lose the end of the thread’s name, and keep how the shape sews and its number. Here Column 1, Russet Brown shows as Column 1, Russet Bro. Widen the panel to read it whole. Search All still finds it by its whole name.

The names are written once, when the picture is traced, and are then the shapes’ own names, as a name you give a shape is. StitchCraft never renames a shape that has a name. Rename any you like. A shape you draw later has no name, and the notices name it for that plan only, with the next free number.

A document traced by an older StitchCraft has names with the thread first, such as Red fill (3). They are its shapes’ own names, so they stay as they are. A shape you draw later gets a name in the new order, with the first number that no other shape’s name has.

In the notices

A notice about a shape names it before its id:

info SC-I0912 ("Fill 3, Red", vc:63:fill): This shape sews by the rule area.

The id, vc:63:fill, is what a bug report needs. The name is what you look for in the Layers panel.

In the report

stitch plan … --report report.json gives each notice about a shape its name, and lists the shapes under shapes in the order they sew. Each has its name, element, thread, kind and place, from

  1. The shapes that sew nothing come last, with no place:
"shapes": [
  { "name": "Fill 1, Red", "element": "vc:6", "thread": "#ed171f", "kind": "fill", "place": 1 },
  { "name": "Left out 1, White", "element": "vc:3", "thread": "#f0f0f0", "kind": "left out", "place": null }
]

The sewing order is chosen again at every plan, and one edit can move many shapes in it. The report gives the order as this plan sews it.

A path’s own Auto effect in an automatic group

StitchCraft’s Auto effect on a group makes everything in the group automatic with its settings. A path inside it with an Auto effect of its own sews by its own settings alone. VectorCraft stores an effect with every setting, so none of the group’s reach the path. SC-W0920 names the path and each of the group’s settings it doesn’t take:

warning SC-W0920 ("Red fill 2", vc:6): The object "Red fill 2" has StitchCraft's Auto effect of its own
inside a group with one, and sews by its own settings alone: it doesn't take the group's auto_overlap_mm
0.6 (its own: 1).

To sew the path by the group’s settings, remove its own Auto effect. To keep it, set those settings on it too.
