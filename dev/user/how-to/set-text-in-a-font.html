StitchCraft

Set text in an embroidery font

stitch lettering sews a text in an embroidery font: a font whose letters were digitized one by one, with their satin columns and running stitches. It reads fonts in Ink/Stitch’s format, where a font is a folder with a font.json and a glyph file for each direction it sews in. Ink/Stitch’s own fonts are in that format, in its embroidery-fonts repository, each with its licence file.

Sew a word

Name the text, the font’s folder and the machine file:

stitch lettering "Hello" --font fonts/small_font -o hello.pes --preview hello.png

The command plans the text for the reference machine, a Brother PE800 with its 5 × 7 in hoop, or for the machine --profile names. It prints the font, the scale, the size of the sewn text, its stitches and its threads, and --preview draws what the file sews. Hello in Ink/Stitch Small Font is 20.2 mm wide and 5.4 mm tall, in 401 stitches.

A text of several lines has \n where each line ends:

stitch lettering "Stitch\nCraft" --font fonts/small_font --align center -o name.pes

Choose the size

A font sews well between 2 heights, which its author set. --scale gives the size in percent of the font’s own, and --height gives it in millimetres, measured on the font’s reference capital:

stitch lettering "Hello" --font fonts/small_font --height 8 -o hello.pes

Ink/Stitch Small Font sews from 3.6 to 15.2 mm tall, Ink/Stitch Medium Font from 11.4 to 22.9 mm, and Barstitch Bold, whose strokes are half again as wide, from 11.5 to 29.9 mm. A size outside a font’s range is set at the nearest size inside it, and SC-W1106 names the range. For smaller or larger letters, choose a font drawn for them.

Make the letters bolder

--widen-percent sews every satin column of the text wider, by that percent of its width on each side. It takes 0 to 50 and is 0 unless you set it. In the Lettering effect’s dialog it is Widen columns. Use it where you want bold letters smaller than a bold font sews, such as Barstitch Bold under 12.5 mm:

stitch lettering "Hexmira" --font fonts/small_font --height 10 --widen-percent 20 -o hexmira.pes

At 20, Small Font’s strokes sew about a fifth of the letters’ height wide, as heavy as a bold font’s. Running stitches and fills sew as they are. Under 8 mm the small holes of the A and the 4 fill with thread first. Keep widened letters 8 mm tall or taller. Look at the preview before you sew. A value outside 0 to 50 is set at the nearest, with SC-W0102.

Space and align the text

--letter-spacing, --word-spacing and --line-height add millimetres between letters, words and lines, or take them away when negative. They are millimetres at a scale of 100 percent and grow and shrink with the text. --align takes left, center, right, block or letterspacing. A block spreads the words of each line as wide as the widest line.

Set the text along a path

Draw the path in an SVG file, give it a stroke, and name the file with --path:

stitch lettering "Hello" --font fonts/small_font --scale 300 --path arc.svg -o arc.pes

The text follows the path from its start, each letter turned the way the path runs under it. The letters aren’t bent. The text lies where the path lies on the file’s page. In a file with several paths, label the one to follow batch lettering, as Ink/Stitch’s batch lettering asks. Without that label the file’s first path is taken, and the summary names it.

  • --text-position takes left, center, right or stretch. The first 3 put the text at the path’s start, at its middle or at its end. Stretched, its letters are spread from the start to the end.
  • --vertical-text-position takes top, baseline or bottom. With top the text stands on the path, and with bottom it hangs from it. With baseline the letters’ baseline is the path, and a p reaches below it.

A text longer than its path goes on straight past the path’s end, and SC-W1109 gives both lengths. Draw the path as the text’s baseline should run, in the direction the text reads. On a tight bend the letters’ tops spread apart or touch: look at the picture, and add --letter-spacing where they touch.

Lines, trims and colours

  • Back and forth. Ink/Stitch Small Font and Medium Font draw every letter twice, to sew from the left and from the right. The second line of a text then sews back from where the first ended, and the thread between the lines is short. This is on unless --back-and-forth false turns it off. Most fonts sew one way. In them every line sews from its start, the thread crosses the text between 2 lines, and SC-W1110 says so.
  • Trims. --trim takes off, line, word or glyph, and adds a trim after each line, each word or each letter. A font’s own trims stay.
  • Colours. A multicolour font sews each letter in its 2 or 3 threads before the next letter, with a thread change each time. --color-sort all sews the whole text’s first colour, then its second. line and word do so for each line or each word. It needs a font that sorts its colours, as its font.json says with sortable.

On a machine that doesn’t cut the thread

The Brother PE800 cuts no thread between letters. Every jump leaves a thread on the fabric to cut by hand, and the summary counts the jumps.

  • Leave --trim off. A trim doesn’t cut anything on this machine. It ends the stitching with a knot and jumps, where the letters of Small Font and Medium Font would have been joined by stitches under their columns. Hello World in Small Font at 100 percent has 3 jumps without trims and 10 with a trim after each letter.
  • Keep back and forth on, and choose a font that sews both ways for a text of several lines. The thread between 2 lines is then as short as the lines are apart.
  • Sew small. Up to about 10 mm tall, a word in Small Font sews without a jump. Above that the gaps between letters grow past 3 mm, and each becomes a thread to cut.
  • Sort the colours of a multicolour font with --color-sort all: each thread change is a stop at the machine, and a sorted text has one for each colour.

What to check

  • A character the font lacks sews as the font’s default glyph, and SC-W1107 names the character.
  • A text wider than the hoop isn’t written: SC-E0701 gives its size and the hoop’s. Set it smaller, on more lines, or for a larger hoop.
  • Threads between letters. At small sizes the letters of a word in Ink/Stitch Small Font are joined by a stitch on the fabric. At larger sizes the needle jumps from letter to letter, and a machine that doesn’t cut the thread, as the PE800, leaves those threads to cut by hand. The summary counts them.
  • The picture. Look at it before you sew, since it draws the file’s stitches as the machine sews them.

The settings are listed in the command-line reference, and fonts describes how a text is laid out and how it follows a path.
