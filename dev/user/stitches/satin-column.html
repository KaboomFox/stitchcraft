Satin column

A satin column sews a band with stitches straight across it, close side by side, so that the thread lies smooth and catches the light. The needle crosses the band, comes back across slanting a little to where the next stitch starts, and so on to the band’s end. It is the stitch of borders, lettering and outlines too wide for a running stitch. Past about 7 mm its stitches lie loose and can snag (embroidery basics), so a wider band is split or filled.

Turn an element’s satin_column on, and draw the band in one of 2 ways:

  • One path with a stroke. The path is the column’s middle, and the column is as wide as the stroke. The stroke’s joins and caps shape its corners and ends.
  • 2 rails. 2 subpaths along the band’s edges, and rungs, short lines across both, where the band turns. The stitches run between the rails, at the angle the rungs give them. Satin columns and presets in VectorCraft shows how to draw both in VectorCraft, and how to make a line into rails and rungs.

satin_method chooses how the band is sewn. A satin column is the default, and each other method has a page of its own among the stitch types. The VectorCraft plug-in sets these settings, and StitchCraft reads them from an SVG file from milestone M8 (setting parameters).

The pictures are drawn from docs/fixtures/zigzag.svg, a wave drawn 3 mm wide as one path, and docs/fixtures/s-stitch.svg, a curved band 6 mm wide between 2 rails.

Drawn as one path

A path with a stroke sews as a column as wide as the stroke, its middle along the path. Its rails lie half the stroke’s width to each side of the path, and rungs cross it at the path’s nodes and on either side of each sharp corner, where the stitches fan round it. Automatic digitizing sews a stroke this way when it is wider than the design’s narrowest satin stroke and no wider than the widest satin column (automatic digitizing).

Spacing

zigzag_spacing_mm is how far apart the stitches across are, along the band, 0.4 mm by default. The needle comes back slanting to where the next stitch across starts, a spacing along. A finer spacing covers the band more closely, and a wider one lets the fabric show through.

Underlays

An underlay is a few stitches sewn first, under the column. It holds the fabric still, so that the top stitches sew where they are drawn, and lifts them so that they cover the fabric. A satin column has 3, each off by default, and sews those that are on in this order:

  • Centre walk (center_walk_underlay): a running stitch along the middle of the column, there and back. It suits a narrow column.
  • Contour (contour_underlay): a running stitch along each edge, a little inside it. It keeps the edges crisp.
  • Zigzag (zigzag_underlay): a sparse zigzag across the column, to its end and back. It makes a wide column look fuller.

Automatic digitizing chooses them by the column’s width (automatic digitizing). The pictures sew the band’s top stitches 1 mm apart, so that the underlay shows between them.

Compensation

As it sews, the thread pulls the fabric in across the stitches and pushes it out along the column. A column then comes out narrower and longer than drawn. pull_compensation_mm lengthens each stitch past the rails, and push_compensation_mm shortens the column at its ends. Each takes 2 values, one for each side or end. Automatic digitizing gives its columns 0.4 mm of pull compensation.

Long and short stitches

With max_stitch_length_mm set, a stitch longer than it is split, and split_method says where the splits go. The S-stitch page shows the split methods. On the inside of a tight curve, the needle points along a rail crowd together and the thread piles up. A satin column moves some of them in along their stitches, by default, to spread them out: short_stitch_inset says how far, and short_stitch_distance_mm which points crowd.

Every satin method warns of stitches that lean far along the band and of stitches long enough to snag, as the S-stitch page describes.
