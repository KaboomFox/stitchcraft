StitchCraft

Roadmap: small steps to the final project

Each step is one pull request: small, reviewable, with its tests, conformance cases and docs. Each milestone ends with green gates and, where it produces something sewable, a machine checkpoint (MC) on the reference Brother PE800 with photos and measurements filed through the sew-out report form (machine testing).

Step ids are stable (M3.4); PR titles start with them. Status lives in ROADMAP.md at the repository root and is updated in the same PR that completes a step.

Legend: ✅ done in the bootstrap commit · 🧪 spike (answers a question; may produce a decision instead of code) · 🧵 machine checkpoint · 👤 owner action

M8 — Ink/Stitch interoperability

Goal: a file made with Ink/Stitch sews in StitchCraft as Ink/Stitch sews it, and opens in VectorCraft as a document that sews the same. The compatibility contract lists what Ink/Stitch reads in a file to sew it, and the milestone in which StitchCraft reads each setting. The Ink/Stitch differential (conformance) sews each step’s cases in both programs, and from M8.6 the glyphs of Ink/Stitch’s fonts too. Ink/Stitch’s source is read at the commit the differential pins, and its behaviour goes into the design pages in StitchCraft’s own words (ADR-0012).

Each glyph is an automatic element, its contours filled by the nonzero rule and digitized by M13’s rules at that height. M13’s rules for specks and the background don’t apply to glyphs, and every contour sewsits requirements active. The corpus’s glyphs digitized at 3 heights, with their jumps, filled parts and columns shorter than 1 mm counted as the baseline the next steps are measured against
M15.4A sample sheet. stitch font sample sews a font’s glyphs at its least and greatest heights, with pangrams and the font’s kerning pairs. It reports each glyph’s stitch types, jumps and warnings. TS-15 is drawnthe docs show a sample sheet in pictures
M15.5Dots and marks. A part of a glyph whose centre line has no length, such as an i-dot, a full stop or an umlaut’s dot, sews as one satin column across it. A part too small for a column sews as stitches back and forth inside it, with its locks inside itits requirements active, and every dot of the corpus’s glyphs sewn at the least heights measured
M15.6Ends and serifs. A stroke cut by the baseline, the x-height, the capitals’ height or the descenders’ line ends flat along that line, its stitches turning to lie along the cut. A serif narrower than 0.8 mm sews as part of its stem’s column, and a broader one as a column of its own under the stemits requirements active, and fewer columns shorter than 1 mm in the corpus’s serif fonts than at M15.3
M15.7Thin strokes, counters and small letters. A stretch of a column narrower than the narrowest satin column in lettering is widened to it, or runs along its centre line, as the step’s cases and MC-10 decide. That width is a setting of its own, its default chosen at MC-10. Columns in small letters take lighter settings, as small fonts digitized by hand do: denser satin, a lighter underlay and less pull compensation. Pull compensation leaves each counter at least 0.9 mm across, and a warning names the glyphs whose counters close at the height setits requirements active
M15.8Routing inside a glyph. A glyph’s columns and lines are joined by runs under the columns sewn after them, as M10’s auto-satin routing joins columns. Each glyph starts at its lower left and ends nearest the next glyph. Jumps are left only where a run can’t be hidden, and they have locks at both endsits requirements active, and the jumps inside the corpus’s glyphs counted against M15.3’s
M15.9 🧪Scripts and overlapping strokes. The letters of a joined script sewn as one shape per word, or glyph by glyph with each join ending under the next letter. A variable font’s overlapping contours sewn as strokes of their own, or united. Both measured on the corpus’s script fontsdecision recorded in the design
M15.10Heights. For each glyph, the heights over which its stitch types don’t change. For a font, the range in which its narrowest strokes sew and its widest are satin columns. stitch lettering warns outside the range, as for an embroidery font. The step also chooses whether the height sets a variable font’s weight or optical sizeits requirements active, and the corpus’s ranges recorded beside those of the Ink/Stitch fonts made from the same fonts
M15.11Ported fonts written in Ink/Stitch’s format. stitch font port Font.ttf writes a font folder digitized at a height. Each glyph has a layer of its own, in which satin columns are drawn with their rails and rungs. The settings give the font’s advances, its kerning and its range of heights. The folder has the font’s copyright and licence, and names the font it was made from. It is written only for a font whose licence allows a modified version, or one whose licence the user confirms. Its name leaves out the font’s Reserved Font Namesread back by M14’s reader, the folder sews as the port did, and Ink/Stitch sews it in the nightly differential
M15.12VectorCraft. A text object without StitchCraft’s effects sews in its own font, ported automatically, in stitch export --auto. Its font is found among the fonts installed and those of craft-fonts, as VectorCraft finds them. A font that isn’t found is a coded error that suggests Type › Create Outlines, whose outlines the Auto effect digitizes. The exporter plug-in can’t read font files, and its text waits for VectorCraft to give plug-ins type as glyph outlines (RFC, P9)a Level B case, in which a document with text exports through stitch export and sews its text ported
