# Roadmap status

The plan, with every step and its done-criteria, is in [docs/src/plan/roadmap.md](docs/src/plan/roadmap.md).
This file is the status board: update it in the same PR that completes a step.

**Where we are (2026-10-08):** M1's code and docs are done: StitchCraft writes PES and DST files, the
reference pages are generated from the code, and the MC-1 test sheets (TS-01, TS-02, TS-10A/B/C) are out
for sewing on the reference machine. M2 is under way: StitchCraft reads PES, PEC and DST files back
(`stitch inspect`), every writer/reader pair round-trips, pinned pyembroidery cross-checks every golden
file, `stitch preview` draws exactly what will sew, `stitch convert` moves designs between PES and DST,
the docs show generated pictures of every test sheet, and the readers are fuzzed for an hour every night.
Public APIs, line coverage and mutation testing now have recorded baselines that only move one way. M2
is complete. M3 is under way. The registry's declarations generate the parameters' reference pages and
their JSON Schema, and their status in the Ink/Stitch contract; every diagnostic code has a case;
StitchCraft reads SVG files into its design model, reporting whatever it leaves out; and the engine
stitches running stitches: even spacing between corners, patterns of lengths, curves followed within the
tolerance, no stitch shorter than the shortest stitch, measured straight, repeats, bean stitch and random
length, manual stitches placed by hand, and lock stitches of every shape at either end of the
stitching. It assembles a design's elements into one plan, with jumps, trims, stops, thread changes and
locks where `ties` says, and fits the plan to the machine. `stitch plan design.svg -o design.pes` takes an
SVG of strokes to a machine file, with a preview and a report. `stitch bug-report` writes one file that
reproduces a problem, and `stitch plan` writes one by itself when StitchCraft finds a bug in itself. The
conformance report shows every active requirement green. Next are the sew-out reports for MC-1, which
closes M1, and for MC-2, which closes M3. The MC-2 sheets are drawn as designs and planned by the engine.
M4's steps are done. A satin column's path is read as its rails and rungs, or made into them from its
centre line, and its underlays and top stitches are sewn as Ink/Stitch sews them. M5's steps are done too.
Tatami fills are sewn as Ink/Stitch sews them, from the region the drawing shows. The MC-3 kit sews satin
columns of every width from 1 to 10 mm, and each underlay side by side. The MC-4 kit sews tatami fills
inside outlines at 3 pull compensations, squares at 5 spacings and 3 angles, and a fill round holes with a
part apart. The reference machine is a Brother PE800 (ADR 0013), with a profile for each of its 3 hoops,
and TS-02, TS-02B and TS-10 are redrawn to fit them.
M7.1 to M7.9 are done: every satin method and every fill of M7 is sewn. M13, image to embroidery, is under way, so that a
picture traced in VectorCraft sews well without settings on its shapes: with `--auto`, a shape without
settings sews by its width (M13.1). The shapes are then sewn as neighbours (M13.2): the background is left
out and specks merge into the areas beside them. Each area reaches under the shapes sewn after it, or is cut
away under those that cover it. They sew in an order of their own (M13.3). What overlaps keeps its paint
order, and then each thread sews as long as it can. A row of badges drawn badge by badge changes thread
twice instead of 8 times. `--sort-colours` orders the shapes with settings too. A part of a shape narrower
than the narrowest satin stroke everywhere runs along its centre line, which a constrained Delaunay
triangulation gives, and a running stitch is tripled from 0.5 mm wide (M13.4). A part no wider than a satin
column, 7 mm, sews as satin columns along its centre lines, between its own outlines: the column after a
corner or a junction ends under the one that runs on, 0.4 mm longer, and every automatic satin column has the
underlay of its width and a pull compensation of 0.4 mm (M13.5). A part wider than a satin column in places
sews in parts. Its broad parts are filled, and its narrow parts, cut square where they meet them, sew over
the fill as lines and columns (M13.6). TS-13A to TS-13E sew the test pictures, each digitized beside the
same shapes in paint order, for MC-8. In VectorCraft (M13.7), `stitch palette` writes the machine's threads
for Image Trace, each rule's value is a setting of the shape it digitizes, and StitchCraft's Auto effect on
a traced group digitizes it automatically, with those settings in its dialog. It previews each path's
stitches, or its outline when digitizing it costs more than a live effect may spend, and the Test Sheet
filter draws TS-13 too. `stitch plan picture.png` traces a PNG or JPEG file with VectorCraft's command line
in the machine's threads, fitted into the hoop, and sews it digitized automatically (M13.10). Satin columns,
tatami fills and automatic digitizing have their user pages with pictures, so every stitch type sewn has
one (M13.11). The offsetter shrinks a region 150 mm across 300 times in under 200 ms, ready for contour
fill (M7.6). Before a traced picture is digitized, its layers are flattened (M13.12). Each area sews only
what shows of it, and a broad area is filled whole under the details on it, so a picture traced with its
colours stacked sews as one traced with them abutting. A disc or a square no wider than a satin column is a
dot, which stays in the fill. TS-13D sews an emblem and TS-13E a button, each traced both ways. The owner's dog, cut out
of her 2 photos and traced, is a test picture whose cases CI runs in the release build, and the 3 stitches it
still lays astray are listed for M13.17 and M13.18 and its jump threads for M13.17 (M13.15). A design digitized automatically covers fabric of any colour (M13.16). One base layer sews first under all of it, and neighbours overlap by 1 mm. What the top thread still leaves bare is slivers of at most 0.4 mm², with the base layer under them. The stitches of each part of a shape are chosen from its geometry by one table of rules, which fills a blob and a ragged patch where width alone sewed columns, and each shape says which rule chose them (M13.21). The elements sew in the order that changes thread least: the owner's traced dog changes thread 8 times in 6 threads and 12 times in 9, where it changed 12 to 28 times (M13.22). Where shapes sewn later cover a way between 2 shapes of one thread, the needle runs under them instead of jumping: the dog leaves 60 to 83 jump threads where it left 73 to 96 (M13.17). A traced photo whose background is one area of thousands of corners plans within the work budget, where 2 photos stopped with `SC-E0004` (M13.23). Thin, ragged lines that a photo's shading and fur trace into merge into the patches beside them, and the dogs leave 15 to 32 % fewer jump threads (M13.18). A likeness measure counts the parts of each colour that the stitches keep: the traced dogs keep 0.78 to 0.92 of theirs, and the pear, the lettered patch and the test sheets every part (M13.41). With `auto_details_last` on, a small area with neighbours all round it sews after the larger areas round it where no thread change is needed for it. The setting is on by default since one cap limits the thread at every spot (M13.40, M13.45). Contour fills are sewn
as Ink/Stitch sews them, inner to outer or in a spiral or double spiral, and TS-11B samples them for MC-6
(M7.7). A meander fill wanders through the sides of a tiling made in code, and TS-11C samples its 7 tiles
(M7.8). A circular fill sews a spiral round each part's middle, its pieces routed as a tatami fill's rows,
and TS-11B samples it too (M7.9). An Ink/Stitch file sews with its settings, each `inkstitch:*` attribute
of a shape a parameter of its elements (M8.1), and an older file's settings as Ink/Stitch updates them
(M8.3). Its fills and satin columns start and end at its start and end commands, a VectorCraft document's
at its `stitch:start` and `stitch:end` markers, and its circular fills turn round its target point
(M8.4). TS-11A samples the new strokes and satins, so MC-6's sheets are all drawn. M13.8, which
the owner took ahead of MC-8's sew-out on 2026-11-21, digitizes a VectorCraft document automatically by
default, and `--no-auto` sews it as drawn. Next are MC-8, then MC-6, and M8 goes ahead meanwhile. M10 has begun with cross stitch, sewn with each of
Ink/Stitch's crosses plain and flipped, on a grid that may turn, which TS-17A samples for MC-12 (M10.1,
M10.2). Plan assembly sews the groups of one element in threads of their own (M10.5), and a tartan fill
weaves a plaid of rows in the colours of its sett, each in its own thread, which TS-17B samples (M10.6).
A linear gradient fill shares out its stops' colours among its rows, which TS-17B samples too (M10.12).
A guided fill's rows are copies of a line drawn with it, and TS-17B samples one in a leaf (M10.9). They
can also be the line's offset curves or rings round it, which TS-17B samples in a fan and an egg (M10.10).
M10 comes before
M14, since lettering's fonts use its stitch types. M15, planned after M14, ports TrueType and
OpenType fonts for lettering automatically.
